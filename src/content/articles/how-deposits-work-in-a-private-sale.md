# How deposits work in a private home sale

**Short answer:** when an offer is accepted, the buyer pays a deposit to show they're serious. In a private sale it goes into a **lawyer's or notary's trust account** (or a brokerage's, if the buyer has an agent), never to the seller directly. The holder keeps it until closing, when it counts toward the price. If a condition in the offer isn't met, the deposit usually goes back to the buyer. If the buyer simply fails to complete a firm deal, the seller can usually keep it and may claim more. Exactly what happens is whatever the signed agreement says.

---

## 1. How it works, step by step

1. **The offer names the deposit:** the amount, who holds it, and when it must be paid (often within a set number of days of acceptance).
2. **The buyer pays the trust account**, usually by bank draft or wire transfer, and the holder confirms receipt to both sides.
3. **The holder keeps it in trust** while the buyer works through any conditions, such as financing or an inspection.
4. **At closing**, the deposit is credited toward the price, so the buyer pays that much less on the day.

---

## 2. Who holds it in a private sale

With agents on both sides, a brokerage usually holds the deposit. In a private sale there's no listing brokerage, so the buyer and seller agree who holds it, and the offer says so. Common choices:

- **The seller's lawyer** (or notary in BC), in their trust account.
- **The buyer's lawyer or notary.**
- **The buyer's agent's brokerage**, if the buyer has an agent.

Whoever holds it acts as a neutral **stakeholder**. They release it only when both sides agree, or when a court decides.

> **Warning: never pay or accept a deposit directly.** Sellers: don't take cash, cheques or e-Transfers yourself. Buyers: only pay a trust account you've confirmed with your own lawyer, and call to check any wire instructions you get by email before sending money.

---

## 3. How much?

There's no set amount. The buyer and seller agree it in the offer. A larger deposit tells the seller the buyer is serious, and it gives the seller more protection if the buyer fails to complete. Ask your lawyer what's usual where you live.

---

## 4. What happens if the deal doesn't close

| What happens | What usually happens to the deposit |
|---|---|
| The deal closes | Credited to the price at closing |
| A condition isn't met (financing, inspection) and the buyer ends the deal as the offer allows | Returned to the buyer |
| The buyer cancels during BC's 3-day rescission period | The buyer must pay the seller 0.25% of the price; ask your lawyer or notary how it's paid |
| The buyer fails to complete after the deal is firm | Usually goes to the seller, who may also sue for further losses |
| The seller fails to complete | Usually returned to the buyer, who may also have other claims |
| Buyer and seller disagree | The holder keeps it until both agree or a court decides |

These are the usual outcomes, not rules. The signed agreement and your province's law decide, so ask your lawyer before you walk away from any deal.

---

## 5. Tips for sellers

- Tell buyers in your listing or first messages that the deposit goes to a lawyer's trust account.
- Ask your lawyer to confirm in writing when the deposit arrives. Until it does, don't treat the deal as done.
- Keep showing the home until conditions are removed, if your agreement allows it.

## 6. Tips for buyers

- Have your bank draft or wire ready for the deadline in the offer. Missing it can put the deal at risk.
- Make sure the offer says clearly when the deposit comes back to you, for example if your financing isn't approved.
- Confirm the seller owns the home through a title search before you pay.

## Frequently asked questions

**Who holds the deposit when you buy a house privately?**
Whoever the offer names: usually the seller's or buyer's lawyer (or notary in BC) in a trust account, or the buyer's agent's brokerage if there is one. Never the seller personally.

**Do I get my deposit back if my financing falls through?**
If your offer was conditional on financing and you end the deal as that condition allows, the deposit is usually returned. If you'd already removed the condition, you may lose it. Check the wording with your lawyer.

**What happens to the deposit if the buyer backs out?**
If the deal was firm, the seller can usually keep the deposit and may sue for more if their losses are bigger. In BC, a buyer who cancels within the 3-day rescission period pays the seller 0.25% of the price instead.

**Is the deposit part of the down payment?**
Yes. It's credited toward the purchase price at closing, so it counts as part of what you pay.
