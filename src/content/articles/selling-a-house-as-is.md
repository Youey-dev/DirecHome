# Selling a house as-is: what it does and doesn't mean

**Short answer:** selling "as is" means you won't make repairs or give promises about the home's condition: the buyer takes it in the state it's in, usually at a price that reflects that. It does **not** let you hide problems you know about. Across Canada, sellers who conceal known hidden defects that make a home dangerous or unfit to live in can be sued after the sale, "as is" or not. Your lawyer words the as-is terms in the agreement.

---

## 1. What "as is" means in practice

- **No repairs:** you won't fix anything before closing.
- **No warranties:** the agreement says you're not promising the condition of the home or its systems (beyond what the law requires).
- **Buyer does their homework:** buyers usually still inspect; an as-is sale often means they price their offer around what they find.
- **Usually a lower price,** because the buyer takes on the risk and the work.

Common reasons: an estate sale, a home needing major updates, a seller who's moved away, or simply wanting a quicker, simpler sale.

---

## 2. What "as is" doesn't cover

*It doesn't erase your duty to be honest.*

- **Known hidden defects:** if you know about a problem a buyer couldn't find on a reasonable inspection that makes the home dangerous or unfit to live in (BC's regulator calls these material latent defects), you must disclose it. In BC, it must be disclosed in writing before a contract is signed.
- **Misrepresentation:** if you answer a buyer's question falsely, or your listing says something untrue, an as-is clause won't protect you.
- **Active concealment:** painting over water stains or hiding damage can lead to a lawsuit.

> **Ask your lawyer.** How should the as-is terms be worded in my agreement, and how should I disclose the problems I know about?

---

## 3. How to sell as-is privately

1. **List what you know:** every problem, fixed or not. Take the list to your lawyer.
2. **Say "as is" clearly in your listing,** and describe the condition honestly: "needs a new roof", "original kitchen", "sold as is, no warranties".
3. **Price with the condition in mind:** compare with recent sales of similar homes in similar condition; see [How to research your asking price](/guides/how-to-research-your-asking-price).
4. **Welcome inspections:** a buyer who inspects and still offers is less likely to have surprises later.
5. **Clean anyway:** an as-is home that's clean and clear still photographs and shows better.

---

## 4. Should you fix things first instead?

| Fix first if… | Sell as-is if… |
|---|---|
| Repairs are small and cheap (paint, fixtures, cleaning) | Repairs are big (roof, foundation, full renovation) |
| The problem would put off most buyers or lenders | You don't have the time, money or energy |
| You're not in a hurry | Buyers in your area want projects |

Some issues may affect whether a buyer's lender will finance the home, which can narrow your buyer pool. Being upfront helps the right buyers find you.

## Frequently asked questions

**Do I have to disclose defects if I sell as-is?**
Yes, known hidden defects that make the home dangerous or unfit to live in must still be disclosed. "As is" covers the condition a buyer can see or inspect, not problems you hide.

**Can a buyer sue after an as-is sale?**
They can if the seller concealed a known hidden defect or misrepresented the home. Honest disclosure and a properly worded agreement are your best protection.

**Can I sell a house as-is by owner?**
Yes. Say so in your listing, describe the condition honestly, price accordingly and have your lawyer word the agreement.

**Do I need to fix anything before selling?**
No, it's your choice. Small cosmetic fixes often pay off; big repairs may not.
