# Selling a house with tenants: what landlords need to know

**Short answer:** you can sell a rented home, but the tenancy doesn't end just because you sell. The buyer becomes the new landlord on the same terms. If the buyer (or a close family member) wants to move in, there's a set process: in BC and Ontario the notice must be given in a specific way and the tenant gets compensation; in Alberta a monthly tenancy needs three full months' notice. Plan the dates in the sale agreement with your lawyer before you accept an offer.

> **Rules differ by province and change often.** This guide covers BC, Ontario and Alberta, from official sources checked October 30, 2026. For other provinces, check your residential tenancy office, and always talk to your lawyer before giving any notice.

---

## 1. Decide: sell with the tenant, or with vacant possession?

| | Sell with the tenant staying | Sell with vacant possession |
|---|---|---|
| **Good for** | Investor buyers who want rental income | Buyers who want to live there |
| **What happens** | The buyer takes over the lease, deposits and rent | The buyer asks for the tenancy to end, following the rules below |
| **Timing** | Simpler | Notice periods can add months; dates must line up with closing |
| **Watch out for** | Rent and deposit records must be accurate | Disputes, compensation and strict notice forms |

Either way, put it in the agreement: whether the tenant stays, who handles the notice, and how the security deposit is transferred.

---

## 2. Showing a tenanted home

*Respect the tenant's rights; you'll sell faster with their cooperation.*

- **Give written notice before entering** for showings, as your province's tenancy law requires. In Alberta, entering without at least 24 hours' written notice is an offence. In BC, give written notice or get the tenant's permission before showings or open houses.
- **Agree a schedule** with the tenant where you can: set viewing windows, and keep groups small.
- **Ask, don't assume,** about photos of the tenant's belongings; many tenants prefer photos before they move in or with personal items removed.
- **Keep it safe:** see [How to show your home safely](/guides/how-to-show-your-home-safely).

## 3. British Columbia

*From BC's Residential Tenancy Branch, "Sell a rental property".*

- **The tenancy continues** with the buyer as the new landlord. You can't end a tenancy just because you want to sell.
- **If the buyer or a close family member will move in,** the buyer gives you a **written request** before taking possession, and you serve a **Three Month Notice** to end the tenancy (or the buyer serves one after possession).
- **Use the government portal:** these notices must be generated through the Residential Tenancy Branch's landlord-use web portal; notices made another way aren't enforceable.
- **The tenant can dispute** a three-month notice within **21 days**.
- **Compensation:** the tenant gets **one month's rent**, paid on or before the notice's effective date (or they can skip the last month's rent).
- **Deposits:** whoever owns the home when the tenant moves out is responsible for returning the security and pet deposits, so deal with the transfer in your sale contract.

---

## 4. Ontario

*From the Landlord and Tenant Board.*

- **The tenancy continues** after a sale unless it's ended properly.
- **If the buyer (or their spouse, child, parent, spouse's child or parent, or a caregiver) will move in,** the landlord can give the tenant an **N12 notice**, but only if the building has **3 or fewer residential units** and an **agreement of purchase and sale has been signed**.
- **Notice:** at least **60 days**, ending on the last day of a rental period or the end of a fixed term.
- **Compensation:** generally **one month's rent** by the termination date, or another acceptable unit. Ontario's rules changed for some notices given from September 21, 2026, so check the Landlord and Tenant Board's current guidance with your lawyer.
- **The tenant can dispute** at a hearing. If the tenant doesn't leave, the landlord applies to the Board; the person moving in files an affidavit.
- **Bad faith:** if the tenant leaves and the named person doesn't move in, the tenant can apply against the landlord.

---

## 5. Alberta

*From the Centre for Public Legal Education Alberta.*

- **A fixed-term lease continues** until it ends; a sale alone doesn't end it.
- **A periodic (monthly) tenancy** can be ended only once all the sale's conditions are met **and** the buyer asks in writing for the tenancy to end. The notice must be **3 full tenancy months** (for example, notice given June 16 ends September 30).
- **Entry for showings:** at least **24 hours'** written notice.
- **The new owner** takes over the original landlord's obligations, including the security deposit.

---

## 6. Your sale agreement

> **Ask your lawyer.** Before you accept an offer: should the tenant stay, or should we sell with vacant possession? Who gives the notice, and when? Do the closing and possession dates leave enough time for the notice period and any dispute? How will rent, deposits and compensation be adjusted on closing?

Don't promise a buyer vacant possession on a date the tenancy rules can't deliver. If the notice is disputed, closing can be delayed.

## Frequently asked questions

**Can I sell my house with tenants in it?**
Yes. The tenancy continues and the buyer becomes the landlord, unless it's ended following your province's rules.

**How much notice does a tenant get if the buyer wants to move in?**
In BC, a three-month notice through the government portal; in Ontario, at least 60 days with an N12 (for buildings of 3 units or fewer); in Alberta, 3 full months for a monthly tenancy. Fixed-term leases may run to their end.

**Do I have to pay the tenant compensation?**
In BC, one month's rent for a three-month notice. In Ontario, generally one month's rent for an N12, though rules changed for some notices from September 2026. Check with your lawyer.

**Can tenants refuse showings?**
Tenancy laws let you show the home with proper written notice; tenants can't unreasonably refuse, but you must follow the notice rules. Cooperation works better than conflict.
