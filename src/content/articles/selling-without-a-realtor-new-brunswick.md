# How to sell your house privately in New Brunswick

**Short answer:** you can sell your own home in New Brunswick without a real estate agent. You advertise it, show it and talk price with buyers; a New Brunswick lawyer handles the agreement, the deposit, the title and closing. If your property is still in the old Registry of Deeds, it has to be converted to Land Titles on this sale. The buyer pays a 1% real property transfer tax, on the price or the assessed value, whichever is higher.

This guide covers what's specific to New Brunswick. For the steps that are the same across Canada (photos, safe viewings, advertising), see our [Canada-wide guide](/guides/selling-your-home).

---

## 1. Yes, you can sell your own home in New Brunswick

*Owners selling their own property are exempt from real estate licensing.*

Real estate agents in New Brunswick are licensed by the Financial and Consumer Services Commission (FCNB) under the *Real Estate Agents Act*. The Act exempts a person who doesn't ordinarily trade in real estate and is selling property they own. So you can advertise, show and sell your own home.

You'll need a New Brunswick lawyer for the legal side, as in any sale.

---

## 2. Hire your lawyer first

*Line them up before you list, especially if your property needs converting.*

Your lawyer usually:

- prepares or reviews the agreement of purchase and sale and any counter-offers
- holds the buyer's deposit in trust
- converts your property to Land Titles, if needed (see section 3)
- gets your mortgage payout and pays off your lender
- prepares the statement of adjustments (property tax, condo fees, heating oil)
- registers the transfer with the buyer's lawyer on closing day

Ask for a fixed-fee quote, including any conversion work. Then call your mortgage lender about any prepayment penalty: breaking a closed mortgage early can cost thousands.

> **Ask your lawyer.** Is my property already in Land Titles? If not, what will conversion involve? Do I need a survey? How would you like to receive offers?

---

## 3. Land Titles: does your property need converting?

*Required when a property is sold, if it hasn't been done already.*

New Brunswick has two land registration systems: the older Registry of Deeds and the newer Land Titles system, which guarantees ownership. Land **must be converted to Land Titles when it's sold or mortgaged**. Your lawyer does a final search in the Registry of Deeds and sends the information to Service New Brunswick.

Two things to know:

- **Boundaries:** Land Titles guarantees who owns the property, not its exact boundaries. If your parcel has no current survey plan, the buyer or their lender may want a land surveyor to locate the boundaries.
- **Timing and cost:** conversion adds lawyer's work and Service New Brunswick fees. Ask your lawyer early so it doesn't delay closing.

---

## 4. Research your price

*Your assessment matters more in New Brunswick than in most provinces.*

Service New Brunswick assesses every property in the province. Use your assessment as one reference alongside recent sales of similar homes nearby, similar homes listed now, and a paid appraisal if you want an independent opinion. Pricing is your decision.

**Why the assessment matters to your buyer:** the real property transfer tax is 1% of the price **or the assessed value, whichever is higher**. If your home's assessment is above your asking price, your buyer pays tax on the assessment.

> **New Brunswick note: 2026 assessment freeze.** For the 2026 tax year, the value used for property tax is frozen at its 2025 level. A sale is an exception to the freeze, so your buyer's taxable value, and taxes, can change after they buy.

## 5. New Brunswick documents to gather

| Document | Why New Brunswick buyers ask for it |
|---|---|
| Property assessment and tax notice (Service New Brunswick) | Part of the statement of adjustments; affects the buyer's transfer tax |
| Mortgage details | Your lawyer requests the payout statement |
| Deed, survey and any old title documents | Helpful for Land Titles conversion |
| **Heating oil details** | Many New Brunswick homes heat with oil; buyers and insurers ask about the tank's age and location |
| Well and septic records (rural homes) | Lenders and buyers commonly ask for a water test and septic information |
| Condo documents (condos only) | Declaration, bylaws, budget and reserve fund study |
| Permits and receipts for major work | Buyers and inspectors ask |

For the full stage-by-stage list, see our [paperwork checklist](/guides/paperwork-to-sell-your-house-privately).

**Disclosure.** New Brunswick doesn't require private sellers to use a standard disclosure form, but across Canada, hiding a known hidden problem that makes a home dangerous or unfit to live in can lead to a lawsuit after the sale. Write down every problem you know of and go through it with your lawyer.

## 6. If you're married: your spouse joins in the sale

*Even if only you are on title.*

Under New Brunswick's *Marital Property Act*, no spouse can sell or mortgage an interest in a marital home unless the other spouse **joins in the deed** (or consents, where there's no written instrument), with a few exceptions such as a domestic contract or a court order. The Act applies to married spouses; it doesn't cover unmarried partners.

Tell your lawyer early if you're married, separated, or the home is in one name only.

Separating or divorcing? See [Selling your house during a separation or divorce](/guides/selling-your-house-during-separation-or-divorce).

## 7. Offers and transfer tax

*Every offer goes to your lawyer before you respond.*

The agreement of purchase and sale sets out the price, deposit, included items, conditions and the closing date. Common conditions are financing, a home inspection and, for rural homes, water and septic tests.

- You can accept, counter or decline.
- Once both sides sign and the buyer's conditions are met or removed, the sale is firm.
- New Brunswick doesn't have a BC-style 3-day buyer cancellation right for resale homes, so get legal advice before you sign.

**Real property transfer tax (paid by the buyer).** 1% of the price or the assessed value, whichever is higher. On a home assessed at $300,000 that sells for $350,000, it's $3,500. If it sold for $280,000, the tax would still be $3,000, based on the assessment.

> **Warning: deposits.** The buyer's deposit goes into a lawyer's (or brokerage's) trust account, as the agreement says. Never accept a deposit yourself, by cash, cheque or e-Transfer.

---

## 8. Closing in New Brunswick

- A few days before closing, sign the deed (with your spouse, if married) and closing papers at your lawyer's office. Bring government photo ID.
- On closing day, the lawyers exchange documents and money, the transfer is registered, and your lawyer pays off your mortgage.
- Hand over keys and codes as the agreement says; note the oil level if it's being adjusted.
- Your lawyer sends you the net proceeds and a final report.
- At tax time, report the sale on Schedule 3 and designate the home as your principal residence.

## Frequently asked questions

**Can I sell my house without a real estate agent in New Brunswick?**
Yes. The *Real Estate Agents Act* exempts owners selling their own property. You'll still need a New Brunswick lawyer to handle the agreement, Land Titles and closing.

**How much is the land transfer tax in New Brunswick?**
1% of the purchase price or the assessed value, whichever is higher. The buyer pays it when the deed is registered.

**Why does my assessment matter when I sell?**
Because the transfer tax uses the higher of the price and the assessment. If your assessment is above the sale price, the buyer pays tax on the assessment. A sale is also an exception to the 2026 assessment freeze.

**Does my spouse have to sign if the house is in my name only?**
If you're married, yes: under the *Marital Property Act*, your spouse must join in the deed, unless an exception like a domestic contract or court order applies.

**What does it cost to sell a house privately in New Brunswick?**
Mainly your lawyer's fee and any Land Titles conversion work, your mortgage discharge and any prepayment penalty, plus advertising (for example, $24.99 a month on OwnersMarket). If your buyer uses an agent, that agent may ask you to pay their commission, which would reduce your savings.
