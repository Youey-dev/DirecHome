# How to sell a condo privately in Canada

**Short answer:** selling a condo (strata, in BC) privately works like selling a house, plus one extra job: getting the **condo corporation's documents** to buyers. Almost every buyer makes their offer conditional on reviewing them. Each province has its own certificate, with legal deadlines and fee caps: the **Form B** in BC, the **status certificate** in Ontario, the **estoppel certificate** in Alberta and Saskatchewan. Order early, check your building's rules on signs and showings, and send every offer to your lawyer.

---

## 1. The certificate buyers need, by province

| Province | Document | Deadline | Maximum fee |
|---|---|---|---|
| **BC** | Information Certificate (**Form B**), with rules, budget and the latest depreciation report attached | 7 days | $35 plus up to 25¢ a page |
| **BC** (before completion) | Certificate of Payment (**Form F**) | 7 days | $15 |
| **Ontario** | **Status certificate** | 10 days (after request and payment) | $100, including tax |
| **Alberta** | **Estoppel certificate** | 10 days (after written request) | $200 (rush fee may apply if bylaws allow) |
| **Saskatchewan** | **Estoppel certificate** | Ask your corporation | Up to $200 |
| **Other provinces** | Condo corporation documents (declaration, bylaws, budget, reserve fund study) | Ask your corporation or property manager | Ask |

Faster "rush" service often costs more. Ask your property manager how to order.

---

## 2. Other condo documents buyers ask for

- **Bylaws and rules:** pets, rentals, renovations, parking, storage
- **Budget and financial statements**
- **Reserve fund study** (called a depreciation report in BC)
- **Recent meeting minutes:** buyers look for upcoming repairs, disputes and special assessments
- **Insurance summary:** what the building covers, and the deductible owners may face
- **Your unit's details:** parking stall and locker numbers, monthly fees and what they include

---

## 3. Know your building's rules before you list

- **Signs:** many buildings restrict for-sale signs in windows or common areas. Check before you put one up; OwnersMarket's window sign is an option where allowed.
- **Showings:** some buildings require visitors to sign in or limit open houses. Follow the rules and meet viewers at the door.
- **Lockboxes and fobs:** never leave a fob or key in a lockbox in a shared lobby.
- **Move-out:** book the elevator and loading area early for moving day.

---

## 4. Pricing a condo

Compare with recent sales **in your building** first, then similar buildings nearby. Floor, view, layout, parking, storage and monthly fees all affect value. Upcoming special assessments in the minutes can affect what buyers will pay; be ready to talk about them. See [How to research your asking price](/guides/how-to-research-your-asking-price).

---

## 5. Disclosure

Disclose known problems with your unit, and don't hide what you know about the building (for example, a special assessment that's been approved). In BC, known material latent defects must be disclosed in writing before a contract is signed. Your lawyer can help with the wording.

---

## 6. Offers and closing

- Expect a **condition to review condo documents** in nearly every offer; your lawyer explains the deadline.
- Your condo fees and any special assessment are adjusted on closing, based on the certificate.
- Hand over every key, fob, remote and parking pass on possession day.

## Frequently asked questions

**Can I sell my condo without a realtor?**
Yes. You'll need the condo corporation's documents for buyers and a lawyer (or notary in BC) for the legal side, as in any sale.

**Who pays for the status certificate in Ontario?**
Whoever requests it. Buyers often order it during their condition period, but some sellers order one in advance to speed things up. The maximum fee is $100, including tax.

**How long does a Form B take in BC?**
The strata corporation must provide it within 7 days of the request, for up to $35 plus copying costs.

**What is an estoppel certificate?**
In Alberta and Saskatchewan, it confirms your condo fees and anything owing to the corporation. In Alberta, it must be provided within 10 days of a written request, for up to $200.
