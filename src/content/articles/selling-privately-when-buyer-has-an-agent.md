# Selling privately when the buyer has an agent

**Short answer:** it's common, and it's your choice. When a buyer brings a real estate agent to your private sale, the agent works for the buyer, not you, and will usually ask you to pay their commission. You can agree, negotiate a different amount, or say no and leave it between the buyer and their agent. Whatever you decide, agree it in writing with the agent's brokerage before the viewing, and have your lawyer look at it first.

---

## 1. Why it happens, and why it isn't bad news

*Buyers with agents are often serious, pre-approved buyers.*

Many Canadian buyers search with an agent. When one of them finds your home, the agent will usually call to book a viewing and raise the question of their commission. That's normal, and it isn't a sign you're doing anything wrong.

Being open to buyers' agents can widen your pool of buyers. It also reduces what you save, so it's worth deciding your approach **before** the first call, not on the doorstep.

---

## 2. Your three options

*There's no right answer; it depends on your price, your market and your priorities.*

1. **Agree to pay the buyer's agent commission** at the amount they ask.
   - *Upside:* more buyers can consider your home without extra cost to them.
   - *Downside:* the biggest cut to your savings.
2. **Negotiate the amount**, for example a lower percentage or a fixed dollar amount.
   - *Upside:* keeps the door open while protecting more of your savings.
   - *Downside:* some agents may steer buyers to homes that pay more.
3. **Decline**, and leave the agent's pay between the buyer and their agent.
   - *Upside:* keeps the full saving.
   - *Downside:* some buyers won't want to pay their agent themselves, so you may lose some interest.

In Canada, commissions aren't set by law and are negotiable. BC's real estate regulator notes that buyers' agents are usually paid from the seller's side, but that any commission amount can be negotiated. In a private sale, you're the one negotiating it.

> **Estimate only.** On a $650,000 home, the buyer's-agent half of an example commission (7% on the first $100,000 plus 2.5% on the rest, plus GST) is about $10,894. Commissions are negotiable and vary by province. Try the [calculator](/calculator) for your own numbers.

## 3. What the buyer's agent can and can't do for you

*They represent the buyer. You represent yourself.*

When you deal with a buyer's agent without an agent of your own, you're an **unrepresented** (or **self-represented**) party. Provincial regulators require agents to make this clear:

- In **BC**, the agent must give you a *Disclosure of Risks to Unrepresented Parties* form and the regulator's "Not a client? Know the risks" information.
- In **Ontario**, the agent must give you RECO's *Information and Disclosure to Self-represented Party* form, go through it with you, and have you confirm you received it.
- Other provinces have similar rules about dealing with people who aren't clients.

What these forms explain, in short:

| The buyer's agent **can** | The buyer's agent **can't** |
|---|---|
| Book and attend the viewing | Advise you on price or contract terms |
| Share factual information | Negotiate on your behalf |
| Present the buyer's offer to you | Keep your confidential information from their client |
| Fill in their standard offer form for the buyer | Act as your agent at the same time |

**Signing the disclosure form doesn't make you their client;** it confirms you understand you're not one.

> **Keep your bottom line to yourself.** Anything you tell the buyer's agent, such as why you're moving, how fast you need to sell, or the lowest price you'd take, can be passed to their client. That's their job.

---

## 4. If you agree to pay, put it in writing first

*Before the viewing, with the brokerage, reviewed by your lawyer.*

Agents are paid through their **brokerage**, so any agreement to pay a buyer's agent commission should be with the agent's brokerage, in writing, before the viewing. Make sure it says:

- **who** you'll pay (the brokerage's name) and **how much** (a percentage or a fixed amount, and whether tax is extra)
- that it's **only payable if the sale completes** with that buyer
- that it's **paid on closing from the sale proceeds** through your lawyer
- **how long** it applies (for example, only to this buyer, for offers made within a set period)

Have your lawyer read it before you sign. Some brokerages will have their own form; that's fine, as long as your lawyer is comfortable with it.

**Use the right words.** Say "buyer's agent commission", not "finder's fee". Real estate licensees are paid through their brokerage, and paying unlicensed people for finding buyers is restricted.

## 5. When the offer comes through an agent

*Same rule as always: your lawyer sees it before you sign.*

- The buyer's agent will often write the offer on their brokerage's standard form. Send it to your lawyer before you respond, even if it looks familiar.
- Check that the offer matches anything you agreed about commission, and that it doesn't add new obligations for you.
- The deposit may be held in the buyer's brokerage's trust account; that's common. Never take a deposit yourself.
- You can still counter or decline. The agent will present your response to their client.

> **Ask your lawyer.** Does this offer, or anything the agent asked me to sign, commit me to paying commission? Is the deposit going to a proper trust account?

---

## 6. When an agent wants to list your home instead

*You can say no.*

Private listings often get calls from agents offering to list the home for you. That's allowed, and some sellers find it useful, but you don't have to engage. If you're not interested, a polite "No thanks, I'm selling privately, but buyers' agents are welcome to book a viewing" is enough.

Don't sign anything you haven't read. A **listing agreement** makes that brokerage your agent and usually commits you to a commission on any sale during its term, and sometimes after it ends for buyers introduced during it.

## Frequently asked questions

**Do I have to pay the buyer's agent if I'm selling privately?**
No. It's negotiable. You can agree to pay, negotiate a different amount, or decline and leave it between the buyer and their agent. If you agree to pay, put it in writing with the brokerage before the viewing.

**Do buyers pay realtor fees in Canada?**
In most agent-listed sales, the seller pays a commission that's shared with the buyer's agent. In a private sale there's no listing agent, so the buyer's agent's pay has to be agreed separately: either you agree to pay it, or the buyer pays their own agent under their agreement with them.

**Does the buyer need to pay the agent fee if the seller won't?**
That depends on the buyer's agreement with their own agent. Many buyers sign a representation agreement that says what happens if the seller doesn't pay. That's between them; your job is to be clear, early, about what you will and won't pay.

**Can a buyer's agent show my house if I'm selling privately?**
Yes, if you agree. Book it like any viewing, confirm the agent's name and brokerage, and settle the commission question before the visit.

**The agent asked me to sign a form. Should I?**
In BC and Ontario, agents must give you a disclosure form explaining that you're not their client; signing it only confirms you received it. Read anything else carefully, and don't sign a listing agreement or commission agreement until your lawyer has seen it.
