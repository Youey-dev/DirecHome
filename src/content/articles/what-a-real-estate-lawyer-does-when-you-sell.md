# What a real estate lawyer does when you sell your home privately

**Short answer:** your real estate lawyer (or, in BC, a lawyer or notary public) handles the legal side of your sale: they review or prepare the offer, hold the deposit in trust, deal with your title, pay off your mortgage, prepare the statement of adjustments and transfer the home to the buyer on closing day. They don't market your home, set your price or show it. In a private sale they're the professional protecting you, so hire one before you list.

---

## 1. Why every sale needs one, private or not

*An agent sells the home. A lawyer completes the sale.*

In Canada, the legal work of selling a home is done by lawyers (and, in BC, also by notaries public), whether or not there's a real estate agent involved. With an agent, you'd still pay a lawyer. In a private sale, your lawyer becomes even more important, because there's no agent on your side to spot problems in an offer.

The buyer hires their own lawyer. Law society rules generally limit one lawyer acting for both the buyer and the seller in the same sale, so expect two separate lawyers.

---

## 2. Before you list

*A short first call saves stress later.*

Your lawyer can:

- **check your title** for anything that could hold up a sale: an old mortgage that was never discharged, a lien, an easement or a boundary issue
- **tell you what you need to disclose** about known problems with the home
- **list the documents to gather** (see our [paperwork checklist](/guides/paperwork-to-sell-your-house-privately))
- **flag province-specific steps**, like a Real Property Report in Alberta, Land Titles conversion in Nova Scotia or New Brunswick, or spousal consent rules
- **tell you how they'd like to receive offers**, so you can send them on quickly

> **Ask your lawyer.** Is there anything on my title I should fix before I list? What do I need to disclose? How should buyers send offers?

---

## 3. When an offer arrives

*Send every offer to your lawyer before you sign anything.*

- **Reviewing or preparing the agreement.** Often the buyer's lawyer (or the buyer's agent) drafts the offer and your lawyer reviews it. Sometimes your lawyer prepares it. Either way, they explain every term.
- **Explaining the conditions and dates:** financing, inspection, condo documents, the closing and possession dates, and what happens if a deadline is missed.
- **Drafting your counter-offer** or amendments, in proper legal wording.
- **Checking anything else you're asked to sign,** like a buyer's agent commission agreement (see [Selling privately when the buyer has an agent](/guides/selling-privately-when-buyer-has-an-agent)).

What they won't do is tell you whether the price is right. That's your decision. They can tell you what a term means for you, and what your options are.

---

## 4. The deposit

*Money never goes through you.*

The buyer's deposit is paid into a lawyer's trust account (or a brokerage's, if the buyer has an agent), as the agreement says. Trust accounts are regulated: the money is held for the transaction, not by either side. If the deal falls through, the agreement and the lawyers determine what happens to it.

> **Warning:** if anyone asks you to accept a deposit directly, or emails new instructions for where to send money, stop and call your lawyer on the number you already have.

---

## 5. Between the offer and closing

*Your lawyer and the buyer's lawyer do most of the work here.*

- **Title search and requisitions.** The buyer's lawyer searches your title and may send questions or requests (called requisitions) to your lawyer. Your lawyer answers them and fixes any problems.
- **Mortgage payout.** Your lawyer gets a payout statement from your lender and arranges to pay it off from the sale money on closing.
- **Statement of adjustments.** Your lawyer works out who owes what as of closing day: property taxes, condo fees, prepaid utilities, heating oil.
- **Province-specific steps:** spousal consent or dower documents, Land Titles conversion, a Real Property Report, or condo certificates, depending on where you live.
- **Closing documents.** They prepare the transfer (deed) and any declarations, and arrange for you to sign them a few days before closing.

---

## 6. Closing day

*The legal transfer happens; you hand over the keys.*

On closing (called completion in BC), ownership passes to the buyer in exchange for the price:

1. The buyer's lawyer sends the money (usually the buyer's mortgage and their own funds).
2. The transfer is registered at your province's land titles office or registry.
3. Your lawyer pays off your mortgage and any other amounts owing on your title, then registers or arranges the discharge.
4. Your lawyer sends you the net proceeds and a final report showing every amount.

Possession, when the buyer gets the keys, is often the same day but can be different; your agreement says which.

---

## 7. After the sale

- **Your reporting letter** from your lawyer: keep it with your agreement and statement of adjustments.
- **Your tax return:** report the sale on Schedule 3 and designate the home as your principal residence. Your lawyer doesn't file this; your accountant or you do.
- **Mortgage discharge:** your lawyer confirms the discharge was registered, so your old mortgage no longer shows on the title.

---

## 8. What your lawyer doesn't do

| Your lawyer **does** | Your lawyer **doesn't** |
|---|---|
| Review or prepare the agreement and explain it | Market or advertise your home |
| Hold the deposit and move all the money | Set or suggest your price |
| Deal with title, mortgage payout and adjustments | Show the home or talk to buyers for you |
| Prepare and register the transfer | Inspect the home (that's the buyer's home inspector) |
| Handle province-specific legal steps | Do your income tax return |

---

## 9. Choosing a lawyer (or BC notary)

*Hire before you list, not after the first offer.*

**Where to look:**

- Your provincial **law society** has a lawyer directory where you can check a lawyer's status. In BC, the Canadian Bar Association's Lawyer Referral Service offers a low-cost first consultation; Ontario's law society runs its own referral service.
- In **BC**, a notary public can also handle a residential sale.
- Ask friends or neighbours who've sold recently.

**Questions to ask:**

- Do you act for sellers in residential sales regularly, including **private sales without an agent**?
- What's your **fee for a sale**, and what does it include? What **disbursements** (searches, registration fees, couriers) are extra?
- Will you **review an offer quickly**, including on evenings or weekends if needed?
- Who in your office will I deal with day to day?
- Anything **province-specific** I should start on now (a survey, a certificate, a consent)?

Get the quote in writing. Fees vary by province, city and how complicated the sale is, so compare two or three.

## Frequently asked questions

**Do I need a lawyer to sell my house privately in Canada?**
In practice, yes. A lawyer (or, in BC, a lawyer or notary) handles the title transfer, pays off your mortgage and makes sure the money changes hands safely. You'd need one even if you used an agent.

**When should I hire a real estate lawyer when selling?**
Before you list. That way they can check your title and disclosure early, and you can send them an offer the day it arrives.

**Can one lawyer act for both the buyer and the seller?**
Generally not. Law society rules restrict one lawyer acting for both sides of the same sale, except in limited situations. Expect the buyer to have their own lawyer.

**Can a notary handle my home sale?**
In British Columbia, yes: a notary public can handle a residential sale. In the other provinces covered here, a lawyer handles it.

**Does my lawyer negotiate the price for me?**
No. Price is your decision, and you negotiate it with the buyer (or their agent). Your lawyer explains the terms, drafts your counter-offer and protects you legally.
