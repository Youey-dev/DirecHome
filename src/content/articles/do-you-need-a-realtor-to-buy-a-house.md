# Do you need a realtor to buy a house in Canada?

**Short answer:** no. The Financial Consumer Agency of Canada says using a realtor to buy a home is optional. What you do need is a **real estate lawyer** (or, in BC, a lawyer or notary) to review your offer, hold your deposit in trust, check the title and register the home in your name. Usually the seller pays the buyer's agent, but in a private sale the owner may refuse, and your agreement with your agent may then say **you** pay. Ask before you sign anything.

---

## 1. What you need, and what's optional

| | Needed? | What they do |
|---|---|---|
| **Lawyer** (or notary in BC) | Yes, in practice | Reviews or drafts your offer, holds your deposit in trust, searches the title, arranges your mortgage paperwork, registers the transfer |
| **Mortgage lender or broker** | If you're borrowing | Pre-approval and your mortgage |
| **Home inspector** | Strongly recommended | Checks the visible condition of the home before you commit |
| **Real estate agent** | Optional | Finds homes, arranges viewings, negotiates and fills out paperwork |

---

## 2. What an agent does for a buyer

An agent usually searches for homes, sets up viewings, helps you decide what to offer, negotiates with the seller and handles the offer paperwork. If you buy without one, you do those things yourself, and your lawyer covers the legal side.

Buying without an agent can suit you if you:

- have found the home yourself, for example on a for-sale-by-owner site
- know the area and what similar homes have sold for
- are comfortable talking price directly with the owner
- have a lawyer ready to review the offer before you sign

An agent can be worth it if you're new to the area, buying something unusual, or want someone else to negotiate.

---

## 3. Who pays the buyer's agent?

The Financial Consumer Agency of Canada says the seller pays the realtor's fees when you buy a home. That's how most sales through agents work: the seller's listing brokerage shares its commission with yours.

A private sale is different. There's no listing brokerage, so the owner decides whether to pay your agent anything. Many will, some won't.

- **In BC**, a buyer's agency agreement can say you'll pay your agent's brokerage if the listing brokerage doesn't. The BC Financial Services Authority notes that commissions aren't set by the regulator and that you can usually negotiate the terms.
- **In Ontario**, your representation agreement must set out how your brokerage's pay is worked out. If you can't pay it, your agent may ask the seller to cover it in the purchase agreement.

**Before a viewing:** ask the owner whether they'll pay a buyer's agent, and how much. On OwnersMarket, an "Agents welcome" badge means the owner has already said what they'll pay.

## 4. How to buy a house without a realtor

1. **Get pre-approved** and budget for closing costs as well as your down payment.
2. **Choose your lawyer** (or BC notary) before you find the home.
3. **Find homes** on for-sale-by-owner sites, agent listing sites, classifieds and lawn signs. See [how to find homes for sale by owner](/guides/how-to-find-homes-for-sale-by-owner).
4. **View the home and ask questions**, in writing where you can. See [questions to ask a private seller](/guides/questions-to-ask-a-private-seller).
5. **Check what similar homes sold for** nearby, to decide your offer.
6. **Have your lawyer prepare or review your offer**, with conditions such as financing and inspection.
7. **Pay the deposit into a trust account**, never to the seller. See [how deposits work in a private sale](/guides/how-deposits-work-in-a-private-sale).
8. **Remove your conditions, then close** through your lawyer.

The full walk-through is in [how to buy a house for sale by owner](/guides/buying-a-home).

## Frequently asked questions

**Is it legal to buy a house without a realtor in Canada?**
Yes. Using a realtor is optional. You'll still need a lawyer (or notary in BC and Québec) to transfer the home into your name.

**Do I need a lawyer to buy a house?**
In practice, yes. A lawyer or notary transfers the home into your name, and your mortgage lender will require one. They also review your offer and hold your deposit in trust.

**Does the buyer pay realtor fees in Canada?**
Usually not: the seller usually pays. But if you sign an agreement with a buyer's agent and the seller won't pay them, that agreement may make you pay. Read it, and ask your lawyer if anything is unclear.

**Can I get a lower price if I buy without an agent?**
Possibly. A private seller who doesn't have to pay any commission may have more room on price, but that's for you to negotiate. There's no rule about it.
