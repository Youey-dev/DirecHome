# Questions to ask when you buy a house from a private seller

**Short answer:** without agents in the middle, you ask the owner directly, so ask a lot and **get the answers in writing**. Ask about the sale (why they're selling, the possession date, what's included), the house (the age of the roof, furnace and windows, renovations and permits, leaks, pests or other known problems), the running costs (property tax, utilities, condo fees), and anything specific to a condo or rural home. Then check the important answers yourself, with a home inspection and a title search through your lawyer.

---

## 1. About the sale

- **Why are you selling, and when do you need to move?** It tells you how flexible they might be on price and dates.
- **What possession date would suit you?**
- **What's included?** Appliances, window coverings, light fixtures, sheds, hot tubs, rented equipment such as a hot water tank.
- **Have you had any offers?** They don't have to tell you, but many will.
- **Will you pay a buyer's agent?** Ask this before you bring an agent to a viewing. See [do you need a realtor to buy a house?](/guides/do-you-need-a-realtor-to-buy-a-house)
- **Who's on title, and does anyone else need to agree to the sale?** In some provinces, a seller's spouse must consent even if they're not on title.

## 2. About the house

- **How old are the roof, furnace, hot water tank and windows?** Ask for receipts.
- **What renovations have you done, and were permits taken out?** Unpermitted work can affect insurance and resale.
- **Any leaks, flooding, mould or pests, now or in the past?**
- **Any insurance claims on the home?**
- **Is there an old oil tank, knob-and-tube wiring or other known issue?**
- **What's the foundation like? Any cracks or water in the basement?**
- **Is there a legal suite, or a suite that isn't permitted?**

## 3. Running costs

- **What are the property taxes this year?**
- **What do heating, hydro and water cost in a year?** Ask for a few bills.
- **Is anything rented or financed**, such as a hot water tank, furnace or solar panels? That contract may pass to you.

## 4. For condos and strata homes

- **What are the monthly fees, and what do they cover?**
- **Are there any special assessments, planned or under discussion?**
- **How healthy is the reserve fund, and when was the last study or depreciation report?**
- **What are the rules on pets, rentals and renovations?**
- **Can I see the bylaws, recent minutes and financial statements?** Make your offer conditional on reviewing them. In BC, the seller can get a Form B information certificate from the strata.

## 5. For acreages and rural homes

- **Is the water from a well? When was it last tested?**
- **What kind of septic system is it, and when was it last pumped or inspected?**
- **Who maintains the road, and is access year-round?**
- **Any easements, rights of way, or land-use restrictions?**

---

## 6. Get it in writing, then check it

Ask the seller to answer your questions by message or email, so you have a record. In some provinces, sellers often fill out a standard **property disclosure statement**; ask whether they will. Sellers must generally tell buyers about known hidden defects that make a home dangerous or unfit to live in, but you're expected to find visible problems yourself.

Then check the answers:

- **Home inspection:** make your offer conditional on one, and go along if you can. An inspection is visual only, so it won't find everything.
- **Title search:** your lawyer (or notary in BC) confirms the seller owns the home and shows any mortgages or other charges on it.
- **City or municipality:** ask about permits, zoning and any outstanding work orders.
- **Condo documents:** review them, ideally with your lawyer.

> **Tip:** put the answers that matter most into your offer. For example, if the seller says the furnace was replaced in 2022, your lawyer can add that as a written statement in the agreement.

## Frequently asked questions

**What should I ask a private seller before making an offer?**
Ask why they're selling, the possession date and what's included; the age of the roof, furnace and windows; renovations and permits; known problems such as leaks or pests; and the property taxes and utility costs. For a condo, ask about fees, special assessments and the reserve fund.

**Does a private seller have to tell me about problems with the house?**
Generally, sellers must tell buyers about known hidden defects that make a home dangerous or unfit to live in. Visible problems are for you to find, which is why a home inspection matters. The rules vary by province, so ask your lawyer.

**Can I get a home inspection when buying privately?**
Yes. Make your offer conditional on a satisfactory inspection, the same as with any sale.

**Should I trust the seller's answers?**
Most sellers answer honestly, but check the important ones: a home inspection, a title search, permits with the city, and condo documents.
