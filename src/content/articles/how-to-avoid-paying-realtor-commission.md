# How to avoid paying realtor commission when you sell your home in Canada

**Short answer:** the only way to pay no commission at all is to **sell your home privately and agree with the buyer directly**, with no agent on either side. You can also **pay much less** by using a flat-fee MLS® service or a discount brokerage, or by negotiating your agent's rate. Commissions in Canada aren't set by law; they're negotiable, and the Financial Consumer Agency of Canada says they typically run from about 2% to 6% of the sale price. Whichever route you choose, you still pay your lawyer, and if the buyer has an agent, that agent will usually ask you to pay them.

> **Written by OwnersMarket**, a site where owners list their own homes. Every option below is legal and common; the right one depends on your home, your market and how much time you have.

## 1. What commission usually costs

There's no standard rate. Each agreement sets its own, and GST or HST is added on top. Two common shapes:

- A **percentage of the sale price**, such as 2.5% for the listing agent and 2.5% for the buyer's agent.
- A **tiered rate**, such as 7% on the first $100,000 and a lower rate on the rest, split between the two agents.

**Example only:** on a $700,000 home, a total commission of 5% is $35,000, plus 5% GST ($1,750) in a GST-only province, for $36,750. Your own numbers depend on the agreement you sign. Try them in the [savings calculator](/calculator).

## 2. Five ways to pay less (or nothing)

### Option 1: Sell privately to a buyer without an agent: no commission

You advertise the home, show it and agree on the price with the buyer. Your lawyer and the buyer's lawyer handle the contract and closing. Nobody earns a commission.

- **You pay:** advertising (for example, $24.99 a month on OwnersMarket), your lawyer, and normal selling costs.
- **Trade-off:** you do the work an agent would do, and buyers who already have an agent may expect you to pay that agent (see option 2).
- Read [should I sell my house myself?](/guides/should-i-sell-my-house-myself) first.

### Option 2: Sell privately and pay only the buyer's agent: about half

Many private sales still involve a buyer with an agent. That agent works for the buyer and will usually ask you to pay their commission. You can agree, negotiate a lower amount, or decline. Agree it in writing with the agent's brokerage before the viewing, and show it to your lawyer first.

- **You save:** the listing side of the commission.
- See [selling privately when the buyer has an agent](/guides/selling-privately-when-buyer-has-an-agent).

### Option 3: A flat-fee MLS® listing: a set fee instead of the listing commission

A flat-fee MLS® service is a brokerage that puts your home on the MLS® and REALTOR.ca for a set fee. You still show the home and negotiate, and most buyers who find you this way have an agent you'll be expected to pay.

- **You pay:** the flat fee, plus the buyer's agent.
- Compare services in [ways to sell your home privately in Canada](/guides/ways-to-sell-your-home-privately-in-canada).

### Option 4: A discount brokerage: a lower commission with an agent

A discount brokerage gives you an agent for a reduced commission or fee, usually with fewer services. It suits sellers who want an agent to handle negotiations but not a full-service price.

### Option 5: Negotiate with a full-service agent

Commissions are negotiable. You can ask for a lower rate, a cap, or a lower rate if the agent also brings the buyer. Get quotes from more than one agent, and read the listing agreement's term and holdover clause before you sign.

## 3. What you can't avoid

- **Your lawyer** (or a notary in BC): every sale needs one for the transfer, the deposit and paying off your mortgage. See [what a real estate lawyer does when you sell](/guides/what-a-real-estate-lawyer-does-when-you-sell).
- **Paying off your mortgage**, including any prepayment penalty.
- **Commission you've already agreed to.** If you've signed a listing agreement, it may still apply to a buyer who saw the home during the listing, even after it ends (the "holdover" clause). Check yours before switching to a private sale.

## 4. Which option fits you

| If you… | Consider |
|---|---|
| Want to pay the least and can show the home yourself | Option 1 or 2: sell privately |
| Want agents' buyers to see your home | Option 3: flat-fee MLS® |
| Want an agent to negotiate for you, for less | Option 4 or 5 |
| Are short on time or selling something unusual | A full-service agent may be worth the cost |

## Frequently asked questions

**Can I sell my house without paying any realtor commission in Canada?**
Yes, if you sell privately to a buyer who doesn't have an agent. It's legal in every province and territory. You still pay your lawyer and your own advertising.

**Do I have to pay the buyer's agent if I sell privately?**
No, not unless you agree to. The agent works for the buyer. Many private sellers do agree to pay something, because it widens the pool of buyers; whatever you decide, put it in writing before the viewing.

**Is realtor commission negotiable in Canada?**
Yes. Rates aren't set by law, and the Financial Consumer Agency of Canada notes they're negotiable.

**How much does selling privately save?**
It depends on your price and on whether you pay a buyer's agent. As an example only, a 5% total commission on a $700,000 home is $35,000 plus GST. The [calculator](/calculator) shows an estimate with your own numbers.
