# How much does it cost to sell a house in BC?

**Short answer:** the biggest cost of selling a house in BC is usually the real estate commission, plus 5% GST on it. Commissions aren't set by law and are negotiable. On top of that, every seller pays a lawyer or notary, a fee to discharge their mortgage, and possibly a mortgage prepayment penalty, moving costs and, for strata homes, small fees for the strata's forms. The buyer, not the seller, pays BC's property transfer tax.

> **Estimates only.** The commission figures below use an example rate, not a standard one. Your costs depend on your price, your agreement and your buyer.

## 1. The costs at a glance

| Cost | Who pays | With a full-service agent | Selling privately |
|---|---|---|---|
| Real estate commission | Seller | Yes, usually the largest cost | No listing commission; buyer's agent commission only if you agree to pay it |
| GST on the commission (5%) | Seller | Yes | Only on any buyer's agent commission you pay |
| Advertising | Seller | Usually included in the commission | You pay (for example, $24.99 a month on OwnersMarket) |

| Mortgage discharge fee | Seller | Yes, if you have a mortgage | Yes, if you have a mortgage |
| Mortgage prepayment penalty | Seller | Possibly | Possibly |
| Strata Form B and Form F | Seller (strata homes) | Yes | Yes |
| Property transfer tax | **Buyer** | No | No |
| Moving, repairs, cleaning, staging | Seller | Your choice | Your choice |

## 2. Real estate commission and GST

*Negotiable, and taxed at 5% GST in BC.*

Commissions in Canada aren't set by law. The Financial Consumer Agency of Canada says they're negotiable and typically range from about 2% to 6%, depending on where you live. In BC, commission is subject to **5% GST**. BC's provincial sales tax (PST) doesn't apply to residential real estate services.

Here's an **example only**, using a common structure: 7% on the first $100,000 plus 2.5% on the rest, plus GST.

| On a $900,000 BC home | Estimate |
|---|---|
| Commission at the example rate | $27,000 |
| GST (5%) | $1,350 |
| **Total** | **$28,350** |
| Usually split: listing side | about $14,175 |
| Usually split: buyer's agent side | about $14,175 |

Your agreement may use a different rate, a flat fee or something else entirely. Use the [commission calculator](/calculator) to try your own numbers.

**If you sell privately:** there's no listing side. If your buyer has an agent, that agent may ask you to pay their commission (the buyer's agent side above, plus GST). You can agree, negotiate or decline; see [Selling privately when the buyer has an agent](/guides/selling-privately-when-buyer-has-an-agent).

## 3. Lawyer or notary

*Every BC sale needs one, with or without an agent.*

In BC, a lawyer or a notary public handles the contract review, the deposit, your mortgage payout and the transfer at the Land Title Office. Fees vary by firm, location and how complicated the sale is, and there's no official rate, so get two or three written quotes. Ask what's included and which **disbursements** (searches, registration fees, couriers) are extra.

---

## 4. Your mortgage: discharge and any penalty

*Call your lender before you set your price.*

- **Discharge fee:** most lenders charge a fee to discharge your mortgage, plus a small registry fee your lawyer or notary pays. They come out of the sale proceeds.
- **Prepayment penalty:** if you break a closed mortgage before the end of its term, the penalty is usually the higher of three months' interest or the interest rate differential (IRD), and the Financial Consumer Agency of Canada notes it can cost thousands. Ask your lender for an estimate in writing, and ask whether you can **port** the mortgage to your next home.

More in [Selling a house with a mortgage](/guides/selling-a-house-with-a-mortgage).

---

## 5. Strata homes: Form B and Form F

*Small fees, capped by law.*

- **Form B (Information Certificate):** up to $35, plus up to 25 cents a page for copies; the strata must provide it within 7 days.
- **Form F (Certificate of Payment):** up to $15, within 7 days; needed for the Land Title Office.
- Rush fees may apply if you ask with less than 7 days' notice, and copies of other strata documents (minutes, bylaws, the depreciation report) can add to the cost.

---

## 6. Taxes: who pays what in BC

- **Property transfer tax:** paid by the **buyer**, not you. On a $900,000 home it's about $16,000 (1% on the first $200,000 and 2% on the rest), less any first-time buyer exemption. It matters to you because it's part of the buyer's budget.
- **BC home flipping tax:** may apply to your profit if you've owned the home for less than 730 days. You must file a return within 90 days of the sale, generally even if you owe nothing.
- **Income tax:** usually none on your principal residence if it was your home every year you owned it, but you must report the sale on your tax return.

---

## 7. Other costs to plan for

- **Moving:** movers, truck rental or storage.
- **Getting ready:** small repairs, cleaning, painting, optional staging.
- **Photos:** free if you shoot them yourself; a photographer usually charges a flat fee per shoot.
- **Bridge financing:** if your purchase closes before your sale, your lender may offer a short-term loan, at a cost.
- **Utilities and insurance** until completion day.

## Frequently asked questions

**What are realtor fees in BC?**
There's no set fee. Commissions are negotiable and usually a percentage of the price, sometimes with a higher rate on the first $100,000, plus 5% GST. Ask any agent for their rate and what's included, in writing.

**Do sellers pay property transfer tax in BC?**
No. The buyer pays it when the home is registered in their name. It's 1% on the first $200,000 and 2% up to $2 million, with higher rates above that.

**Is there PST on real estate commission in BC?**
No, not on residential real estate services. Commission on a home sale is subject to 5% GST.

**What are seller closing costs in BC?**
Usually your lawyer or notary, your mortgage discharge fee and any prepayment penalty, strata forms for a strata home, and moving. Commission is extra if you use an agent.

**How can I lower the cost of selling in BC?**
Negotiate any commission, compare lawyer or notary quotes, ask your lender about porting your mortgage to avoid a penalty, or sell privately and decide in advance what you'll offer a buyer's agent.
