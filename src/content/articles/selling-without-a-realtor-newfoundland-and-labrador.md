# How to sell your house privately in Newfoundland and Labrador

**Short answer:** you can sell your own home in Newfoundland and Labrador without a real estate agent. You advertise it, show it and talk price with buyers; a lawyer handles the agreement, the deposit, the title search and closing. The province has no land transfer tax: the buyer pays a fee to register the deed. Because NL uses a Registry of Deeds rather than a land titles system, title searches take longer, so hire your lawyer early and gather your old property documents.

This guide covers what's specific to Newfoundland and Labrador. For the steps that are the same across Canada (photos, safe viewings, advertising), see our [Canada-wide guide](/guides/selling-your-home).

---

## 1. Yes, you can sell your own home

*The province's real estate law exempts owners selling their own property.*

Real estate agents in NL are licensed under the *Real Estate Trading Act, 2019*. The Act doesn't apply to an owner who completes no more than three trades a year in real estate they own. So you can advertise, show and sell your own home.

You'll need a lawyer for the legal side, as in any sale.

---

## 2. Hire your lawyer first

*In a deeds-registry province, the title search is the long part.*

Your lawyer usually:

- prepares or reviews the agreement of purchase and sale and any counter-offers
- holds the buyer's deposit in trust
- searches the history of past deeds to confirm your title (or works with the buyer's lawyer, who usually does the main search)
- gets your mortgage payout and pays off your lender
- prepares the statement of adjustments (property tax, condo fees, heating oil)
- prepares and registers the deed on closing day

Ask for a fixed-fee quote, and ask whether they've acted for a seller with no agent. Then call your mortgage lender about any prepayment penalty: breaking a closed mortgage early can cost thousands.

> **Ask your lawyer.** Are there any gaps in my title history we should fix before I list? Do I need a new survey? How would you like to receive offers?

---

## 3. Why the Registry of Deeds matters

*No government guarantee of title, so the paperwork trail does the work.*

Newfoundland and Labrador registers deeds rather than issuing guaranteed titles. Each sale relies on a lawyer reviewing the chain of past deeds and documents to confirm who owns the property and what's registered against it.

What that means for you:

- **Gather old documents:** your deed, any older deeds, surveys, wills or estate papers if the home was inherited, and discharges of old mortgages.
- **Fix problems early:** a missing discharge, an unregistered estate transfer or a boundary question can take weeks to sort out. Your lawyer can find them before a buyer does.
- **Allow time:** build a realistic closing date into the agreement.

---

## 4. Research your price

*Your assessment is a reference, not a price.*

Outside St. John's, the Municipal Assessment Agency assesses properties, with values set as of January 1 each year. The City of St. John's sets its own market values every two years. Use your assessment as one reference alongside recent sales of similar homes nearby, similar homes listed now, and a paid appraisal if you want an independent opinion. Pricing is your decision.

---

## 5. Documents to gather

| Document | Why NL buyers ask for it |
|---|---|
| **Your deed, older deeds and survey** | The title search relies on the chain of past documents |
| Property tax bill and assessment | Part of the statement of adjustments |
| Mortgage details and old discharges | Your lawyer requests the payout; old mortgages must show as discharged |
| **Heating oil details** | Many NL homes heat with oil; buyers and insurers ask about the tank's age and location |
| Well and septic records (rural homes) | Lenders and buyers commonly ask for a water test and septic information |
| Condo documents (condos only) | Declaration, bylaws, budget and reserve fund study |
| Permits and receipts for major work | Buyers and inspectors ask |

For the full stage-by-stage list, see our [paperwork checklist](/guides/paperwork-to-sell-your-house-privately).

**Disclosure.** NL doesn't require private sellers to use a standard disclosure form, but across Canada, hiding a known hidden problem that makes a home dangerous or unfit to live in can lead to a lawsuit after the sale. Write down every problem you know of and go through it with your lawyer.

## 6. If you're married: the matrimonial home

*Your spouse has rights in the home whatever the deed says.*

Under Newfoundland and Labrador's *Family Law Act*, a matrimonial home is treated as owned equally by both married spouses, no matter whose name is on the deed, unless they've agreed otherwise. The same rule doesn't automatically apply to common-law partners.

In practice, expect your married spouse to sign the sale documents. Tell your lawyer early if you're married, separated, or the home is in one name only.

Separating or divorcing? See [Selling your house during a separation or divorce](/guides/selling-your-house-during-separation-or-divorce).

---

## 7. Offers and registration fees

*Every offer goes to your lawyer before you respond.*

The agreement of purchase and sale sets out the price, deposit, included items, conditions and the closing date. Common conditions are financing, a home inspection, a satisfactory title search and, for rural homes, water and septic tests.

- You can accept, counter or decline.
- Once both sides sign and the buyer's conditions are met or removed, the sale is firm.
- NL doesn't have a BC-style 3-day buyer cancellation right for resale homes, so get legal advice before you sign.

**No land transfer tax.** The buyer pays a fee to register the deed at the Registry of Deeds, and another to register their mortgage: each is $100 plus 40 cents for every $100 of value above $500, roughly 0.4%. On a $300,000 home the deed fee is about $1,300.

> **Warning: deposits.** The buyer's deposit goes into a lawyer's (or brokerage's) trust account, as the agreement says. Never accept a deposit yourself, by cash, cheque or e-Transfer.

---

## 8. Closing

- A few days before closing, sign the deed (with your spouse, if married) and closing papers at your lawyer's office. Bring government photo ID.
- On closing day, the deed is registered, the lawyers exchange documents and money, and your lawyer pays off your mortgage and registers its discharge.
- Hand over keys and codes as the agreement says; note the oil level if it's being adjusted.
- Your lawyer sends you the net proceeds and a final report.
- At tax time, report the sale on Schedule 3 and designate the home as your principal residence.

## Frequently asked questions

**Can I sell my house without a real estate agent in Newfoundland?**
Yes. The *Real Estate Trading Act, 2019* doesn't apply to owners making no more than three trades a year in their own property. You'll still need a lawyer for the title search, the agreement and closing.

**Is there a land transfer tax in Newfoundland and Labrador?**
No. The buyer pays Registry of Deeds fees instead: $100 plus 40 cents per $100 of value above $500 for the deed, and the same for their mortgage.

**Why does selling take longer in Newfoundland?**
Because NL uses a Registry of Deeds, the buyer's lawyer has to review the history of past deeds to confirm the title. Gaps or old undischarged mortgages can take time to fix, so start early.

**Does my spouse have to sign if the house is in my name?**
If you're married, expect so: NL's *Family Law Act* treats the matrimonial home as owned equally by both spouses, whatever the deed says. Your lawyer will confirm what's needed.

**What does it cost to sell a house privately in NL?**
Mainly your lawyer's fee, fixing any title issues, your mortgage discharge and any prepayment penalty, plus advertising (for example, $24.99 a month on OwnersMarket). If your buyer uses an agent, that agent may ask you to pay their commission, which would reduce your savings.
