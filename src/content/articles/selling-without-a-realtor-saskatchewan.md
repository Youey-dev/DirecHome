# How to sell your house privately in Saskatchewan

**Short answer:** you can sell your own home in Saskatchewan without a realtor. You advertise it, show it and talk price with buyers; a Saskatchewan lawyer handles the offer, the deposit, the title transfer through ISC and closing. There's no land transfer tax: the buyer pays ISC a title fee of 0.4% of the property's value. If you're married or in a common-law relationship, your spouse will usually need to consent to the sale under the *Homesteads Act*, even if they're not on title.

This guide covers what's specific to Saskatchewan. For the steps that are the same across Canada (photos, safe viewings, advertising), see our [Canada-wide guide](/guides/selling-your-home).

---

## 1. Yes, owners can sell their own homes in Saskatchewan

*Saskatchewan's real estate licensing law exempts owners selling their own property.*

Real estate agents and brokerages in Saskatchewan are registered with the Saskatchewan Real Estate Commission under *The Real Estate Act*. The Act specifically exempts a trade by the owner of the real estate, so you don't need to be registered to advertise, show or sell your own home.

You will need a Saskatchewan lawyer for the legal side. Saskatchewan's public legal education society points out that legal advice is especially important when you buy or sell privately.

---

## 2. Hire your lawyer first

*Line them up before you list, so offers can go straight to them.*

Your lawyer usually:

- prepares or reviews the offer to purchase and any counter-offers
- holds the buyer's deposit in trust
- gets your mortgage payout and pays off your lender
- prepares the statement of adjustments (property tax, condo fees, utilities)
- prepares the transfer and homestead documents, and works with the buyer's lawyer to register the change of ownership at ISC's Land Titles Registry

Ask for a fixed-fee quote, and ask whether they've acted for a seller with no agent. Then call your mortgage lender about any prepayment penalty: breaking a closed mortgage early can cost thousands.

> **Ask your lawyer.** Will you use the Law Society of Saskatchewan's standard offer to purchase, or prepare your own? How would you like to receive offers? What should I tell buyers about the house?

---

## 3. Research your price

*Your assessment is a starting point, not a price.*

Most Saskatchewan properties are assessed by the Saskatchewan Assessment Management Agency (SAMA), which revalues every four years. The current values come from the 2025 revaluation, which uses a **2023 base date**, so the market may have moved since. Use your assessment as one reference alongside:

- recent sales of similar homes nearby (type, size, lot, age and condition)
- similar homes listed for sale now, which are your competition
- a paid appraisal, if you want an independent opinion

Pricing is your decision. In smaller towns and rural areas, comparable sales can be few and far between, which makes honest, well-photographed listings even more important.

## 4. Saskatchewan documents to gather

*Mostly the usual list, with a few things rural buyers will ask about.*

| Document | Why Saskatchewan buyers ask for it |
|---|---|
| Property tax notice and assessment | Part of the statement of adjustments, and buyers' budgets |
| Mortgage details | Your lawyer requests the payout statement |
| Utility and heating costs | Winter heating costs matter to Saskatchewan buyers |
| **Condo estoppel certificate** (condos only) | Confirms your condo fees and anything owing. You request it from the condo corporation, which can charge up to $200. Also gather the bylaws, budget, reserve fund study and recent minutes. |
| **Well and septic records** (acreages and rural homes) | Lenders and buyers commonly ask for a recent water test and septic information |
| Road access, gas and power details (rural) | Common questions for acreages |
| Permits and receipts for major work | Buyers and inspectors ask |

For the full stage-by-stage list, see our [paperwork checklist](/guides/paperwork-to-sell-your-house-privately).

**Disclosure.** Saskatchewan doesn't require private sellers to use a standard disclosure form, but across Canada, hiding a known hidden problem that makes a home dangerous or unfit to live in can lead to a lawsuit after the sale. Write down every problem you know of and go through it with your lawyer.

## 5. Married or common-law? Homestead consent

*Your spouse usually has to consent, even if only you are on title.*

Under Saskatchewan's *Homesteads Act, 1989*, the family home can't be sold or mortgaged without the consent of both spouses, even if only one of them owns it. This covers married spouses and common-law partners. The protection ends when married spouses divorce, or when common-law partners have been separated for 24 months.

Your lawyer prepares the consent and arranges for your spouse to sign it properly. Tell them early if you're married, common-law, separated, or the home is in one name only.

Separating or divorcing? See [Selling your house during a separation or divorce](/guides/selling-your-house-during-separation-or-divorce).

---

## 6. Offers in Saskatchewan

*Every offer goes to your lawyer before you respond.*

The offer to purchase sets out the price, deposit, included items, conditions and the possession date. Common Saskatchewan conditions are financing, a building or wiring inspection, the sale of the buyer's current home and, for condos, review of condo documents.

- You can accept, counter or decline.
- Once both sides sign and the buyer's conditions are met or removed, the contract is binding. If a buyer backs out after that, they can lose their deposit and may be sued.
- Saskatchewan doesn't have a BC-style 3-day buyer cancellation right for resale homes, so get legal advice before you sign.

> **Warning: deposits.** The buyer's deposit goes into a lawyer's (or brokerage's) trust account, as the offer says. Never accept a deposit yourself, by cash, cheque or e-Transfer.

---

## 7. ISC fees instead of a land transfer tax

*The buyer's costs are low, which helps your sale.*

Saskatchewan has no land transfer tax. Land titles are run by Information Services Corporation (ISC), and the buyer pays ISC to transfer the title into their name:

- **Title transfer:** 0.4% of the property's value since July 29, 2023, about **$400 for every $100,000**. On a $350,000 home that's about $1,400.
- **Mortgage registration:** a separate ISC fee for the buyer's mortgage, set in bands by the amount borrowed.
- **Your mortgage discharge:** ISC also charges a small fee to register the discharge of your mortgage; your lawyer handles it from the sale proceeds.

As the seller, you don't pay the transfer fee. On the sale of your own principal residence there's generally no income tax on the gain, but you must report the sale on your tax return.

---

## 8. Closing in Saskatchewan

*Your lawyer finishes the sale; you hand over the keys.*

- A few days before possession, you sign the transfer, homestead consent and closing papers at your lawyer's office (bring government photo ID).
- The buyer's lawyer registers the transfer at ISC, and the sale money flows to your lawyer, who pays off your mortgage.
- Hand over keys, garage remotes and codes on possession day, and leave the included items and a clean home.
- Your lawyer sends you the net proceeds and a final report.
- At tax time, report the sale on Schedule 3 and designate the home as your principal residence.

## Frequently asked questions

**Can you sell a house without a realtor in Saskatchewan?**
Yes. *The Real Estate Act* exempts owners selling their own property from registration. You'll still need a Saskatchewan lawyer for the offer, the deposit and the title transfer.

**Is there a land transfer tax in Saskatchewan?**
No. The buyer pays ISC a title transfer fee of 0.4% of the property's value (about $400 per $100,000), plus a separate fee to register their mortgage.

**Does my spouse have to sign if the house is only in my name?**
Usually, yes. Under the *Homesteads Act, 1989*, the family home can't be sold without both spouses' consent, including common-law partners. Your lawyer arranges the consent.

**Is there a standard offer form in Saskatchewan?**
The Law Society of Saskatchewan has a standard form offer to purchase that lawyers often use, and agents have their own. In a private sale, let your lawyer choose and prepare the document.

**What does it cost to sell a house privately in Saskatchewan?**
Mainly your lawyer's fee, your mortgage discharge and any prepayment penalty, plus advertising (for example, $24.99 a month on OwnersMarket). If your buyer uses an agent, that agent may ask you to pay their commission, which would reduce your savings.
