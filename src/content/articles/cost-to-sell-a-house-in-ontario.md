# How much does it cost to sell a house in Ontario?

**Short answer:** the biggest cost of selling a house in Ontario is usually the real estate commission, plus **13% HST** on it. Commissions aren't set by law and are negotiable. Every seller also pays a real estate lawyer, a mortgage discharge fee and possibly a prepayment penalty, plus moving costs. Ontario's land transfer tax is paid by the **buyer**, not the seller.

> **Estimates only.** The commission figures below use an example rate, not a standard one. Your costs depend on your price, your agreement and your buyer.

| Cost | Who pays | With a full-service agent | Selling privately |

## 2. Real estate commission and HST

*Negotiable, and HST makes it bigger than it looks.*

Commissions aren't set by law. The Financial Consumer Agency of Canada says they're negotiable and typically range from about 2% to 6%, depending on where you live. Ontario's regulator, RECO, also says the amount is for you and the brokerage to agree: a percentage, a flat amount, or both.

In Ontario, commission is subject to **13% HST**, which adds noticeably to the total.

Here's an **example only**, using a common structure: 7% on the first $100,000 plus 2.5% on the rest, plus HST.

| On an $850,000 Ontario home | Estimate |
|---|---|
| Commission at the example rate | $25,750 |
| HST (13%) | $3,348 |
| **Total** | **$29,098** |
| Usually split: listing side | about $14,549 |
| Usually split: buyer's agent side | about $14,549 |

**Who pays the commission in Ontario?** Traditionally the seller pays it, and the listing brokerage shares part with the buyer's brokerage. Buyers in Ontario sign their own agreement with their brokerage, which says what happens if the seller doesn't pay the buyer's agent. In a private sale, you decide whether to offer anything; see [Selling privately when the buyer has an agent](/guides/selling-privately-when-buyer-has-an-agent).

## 3. Your real estate lawyer

*Every Ontario sale closes through lawyers.*

Your lawyer reviews or prepares the agreement, handles the deposit and the statement of adjustments, pays out your mortgage and registers the sale electronically. Fees vary, and there's no official rate, so get two or three written quotes. Ask what's included and which **disbursements** (searches, registration, couriers) are extra.

---

## 4. Your mortgage: discharge and any penalty

- **Discharge fee:** most lenders charge one, and there's a small registration cost; your lawyer pays them from the proceeds.
- **Prepayment penalty:** breaking a closed mortgage early usually costs the higher of three months' interest or the interest rate differential (IRD), and it can be thousands. Ask your lender for a written estimate, and ask about **porting** the mortgage to your next home.

More in [Selling a house with a mortgage](/guides/selling-a-house-with-a-mortgage).

---

## 5. Condo sellers: the status certificate

Ontario buyers almost always make their offer conditional on the condo's **status certificate**. The condo corporation can charge no more than **$100** (including tax) and must provide it within 10 days of the request and payment; faster service can cost more. Buyers often order it, but some sellers order one in advance to speed things up.

---

## 6. Taxes: who pays what in Ontario

- **Land transfer tax:** paid by the **buyer**. On an $850,000 home, Ontario's tax is about $13,475; Toronto buyers pay the City's municipal tax on top. First-time buyers can get a provincial refund of up to $4,000.
- **Non-Resident Speculation Tax:** 25%, paid by buyers who aren't Canadian citizens or permanent residents (with some exemptions).
- **Income tax:** usually none on your principal residence if it was your home every year you owned it, but you must report the sale on your tax return. Different rules can apply if you owned it for under a year or rented it out.

---

## 7. Other costs to plan for

- **Moving:** movers, truck rental or storage.
- **Getting ready:** small repairs, cleaning, painting, optional staging.
- **Rental equipment:** if you rent a water heater or furnace, the agreement must say whether the buyer takes over the contract or you pay it out.
- **Photos:** free if you shoot them yourself; a photographer usually charges a flat fee.
- **Bridge financing** if your purchase closes before your sale.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is there HST on real estate commission in Ontario?**
Yes, 13%. On a $25,000 commission, that's another $3,250. Always ask whether a quoted commission includes HST.

**Who pays real estate commission in Ontario?**
Usually the seller, who pays the listing brokerage, which shares part with the buyer's brokerage. In a private sale there's no listing brokerage, so you decide whether to pay any buyer's agent commission.

**Do sellers pay land transfer tax in Ontario?**
No. The buyer pays Ontario's land transfer tax, and in Toronto the municipal one as well.

**What are seller closing costs in Ontario?**
Your lawyer's fee and disbursements, your mortgage discharge fee and any prepayment penalty, payout of any rental equipment, and moving. Commission and HST are extra if you use an agent.

**How much does a real estate lawyer cost to sell a house in Ontario?**
There's no set fee. It depends on the firm and the sale. Get two or three written quotes that list the fee and the disbursements separately.
