# Selling an acreage or rural home privately

**Short answer:** selling a rural home or acreage privately works like any private sale, but buyers and their lenders ask more questions: **water** (well tests and records), **septic**, **boundaries and access**, and **what the land can be used for**. Have recent well and septic information, your survey and any permits ready before you list. Some provinces add their own rules, such as BC's Agricultural Land Reserve and PEI's limits on land held by non-residents.

---

## 1. Water: your well

*Buyers' lenders commonly ask for a recent water test.*

- **Test before you list:** a recent bacteria test shows buyers the water is safe. In Ontario, Public Health Ontario tests private well water for E. coli and coliforms for free (not chemicals). Other provinces have their own testing options; check with your local health authority.
- **Records:** the well's age, depth, flow (if known), and any treatment system.
- **Shared wells:** if you share a well, have the written agreement ready for your lawyer.

---

## 2. Septic

- **Records:** when it was installed, its permit, and when it was last pumped or inspected.
- **Location:** a simple sketch of the tank and field helps buyers and inspectors.
- **Problems:** if you know of a failing system, disclose it; it's the kind of hidden problem buyers can sue over.

---

## 3. Boundaries, access and surveys

- **Survey:** have your survey or plan ready. In **Alberta**, buyers usually expect a current Real Property Report with municipal compliance.
- **Access:** is the driveway on your land? Any shared roads, easements or rights of way? Your lawyer can check what's registered on title.
- **Fences and outbuildings:** buildings near property lines can raise questions; know where they sit.

---

## 4. What the land can be used for

- **Zoning:** your municipality's zoning sets what buyers can do (more homes, a business, livestock). Point buyers to it rather than making promises.
- **BC: the Agricultural Land Reserve (ALR).** If your property is in the ALR, agriculture is the priority use, most properties are limited to one principal residence, and subdivision is tightly controlled. Check with the Agricultural Land Commission's "Is my property in the ALR?" map, and say so in your listing.
- **PEI: land limits.** Under the *Lands Protection Act*, a non-resident (or any corporation) needs approval from IRAC to hold more than 5 acres or more than 165 feet of shore frontage. Mention acreage and frontage in your listing.
- **Saskatchewan:** many sales involve acreages; spouses must consent under the *Homesteads Act*. See the [Saskatchewan guide](/guides/selling-without-a-realtor-saskatchewan).

---

## 5. Who can buy

Canada's federal ban on buying residential property by people who aren't citizens or permanent residents is in place until **January 1, 2027**, unless it's extended again. If a buyer isn't a citizen or permanent resident, their lawyer will confirm whether the ban, or an exception, applies.

---

## 6. Pricing and photos

- **Comparable sales are scarcer** in rural areas; widen your search area and time frame, or consider an appraisal. See [How to research your asking price](/guides/how-to-research-your-asking-price).
- **Photos:** show the land, outbuildings, views and access road, as well as the house. A simple map of the property lines (from your survey) helps.
- **Describe the utilities:** well, septic, power, heating fuel, internet options.

## Frequently asked questions

**Can I sell an acreage without a realtor?**
Yes. It works like any private sale, with more questions about water, septic, boundaries and land use. Your lawyer handles the legal side.

**Do I need a well test to sell my house?**
It's not always required by law, but buyers' lenders commonly ask for one. A recent test helps the sale go smoothly.

**Can I sell ALR land privately in BC?**
Yes. Buyers need to know the property is in the ALR, because agriculture is the priority use and residences and subdivision are restricted. Check the Agricultural Land Commission's map.

**Can a non-resident buy my rural property?**
It depends. The federal ban on non-Canadian buyers runs until January 1, 2027 unless extended, with exceptions; and in PEI, non-residents need approval for more than 5 acres or 165 feet of shore frontage. The buyer's lawyer will confirm.
