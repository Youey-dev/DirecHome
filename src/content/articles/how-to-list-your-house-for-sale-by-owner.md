# How to list your house for sale by owner in Canada

**Short answer:** to list your house for sale by owner, take bright, complete photos, write an honest description with accurate facts (size, taxes, fees, what's included), then advertise it where buyers look: a for-sale-by-owner site, a lawn sign, classifieds and your local network. The MLS itself is only open to realtors, but you can pay a flat-fee brokerage to put your home there if you want that reach.

Here's how to build a listing that gets viewing requests, and an honest look at where to put it.

---

## 1. Get the home ready before you shoot

*The photos can only be as good as the rooms.*

- Clean, clear the counters and put away personal photos, mail and clutter.
- Fix the small things buyers notice: burnt-out bulbs, dripping taps, sticking doors.
- Open blinds, turn on every light, and tidy the front yard and entrance.
- Move cars out of the driveway for the front photo.

Our [step-by-step selling guide](/guides/selling-your-home) covers getting the home ready in more detail.

---

## 2. Take photos that do the selling

*Buyers decide from the photos whether to book a viewing.*

- **Light:** shoot in daylight with the lights on. Overcast days are fine; avoid harsh noon sun for the front.
- **Angle:** hold your phone level at chest height, in landscape (sideways), from a corner of the room so the photo shows most of it.
- **Order:** front of the home first, then the main living area, kitchen, bedrooms, bathrooms, basement, yard, parking and any views.
- **Coverage:** 10 to 25 photos is plenty. Show every main room once rather than one room five times.
- **Honesty:** don't edit out power lines, cracks or a neighbour's building. Buyers will see them at the viewing, and a misleading photo wastes everyone's time.

If photos aren't your thing, a local real estate photographer usually charges a flat fee per shoot; ask for a quote.

---

## 3. Get the facts right

*Accurate facts protect you as much as they help buyers.*

Have these ready before you write anything:

- **Price** (your decision; see "Research your asking price" in our [selling guide](/guides/selling-your-home)).
- **Property type**, bedrooms, bathrooms, year built, lot size and parking.
- **Size:** measure carefully and say how you measured (for example, "finished area above grade, measured by owner"). Don't round up.
- **Annual property taxes**, and **condo fees** with what they include.
- **Heating and typical utility costs.**
- **What's included and excluded:** appliances, window coverings, hot tub, shed, and anything rented (like a water heater).
- **Major updates** with years: roof, furnace, windows, electrical.

Getting a fact wrong in your listing can come back to you after the sale. If you're unsure, leave it out or say "approximately".

---

## 4. Write a description that works

*Plain, specific and honest beats "stunning" and "must see".*

A simple structure:

1. **Opening line:** the type of home and the one or two things that set it apart. *"Three-bedroom bungalow on a quiet crescent, five minutes' walk from the elementary school."*
2. **The layout:** walk the buyer through the home, floor by floor.
3. **What's been updated,** with years, and what's original.
4. **The outside:** yard, garage, parking, storage.
5. **The location:** what's nearby that buyers care about (schools, parks, transit, shops), in minutes rather than adjectives.
6. **How to see it:** *"Request a viewing through the listing; evenings and weekends available."*

**Describe the home, not the buyer.** Phrases like "perfect for a young couple" or "ideal for a Christian family" can come across as excluding people, and human rights laws across Canada protect people from discrimination in housing. Say what the home offers and let buyers decide.

**Avoid:** superlatives you can't back up ("best street in town"), promises about future value, and anything you'd hesitate to repeat to the buyer's lawyer.

> **Example only.** *"Bright two-storey on a corner lot with a fenced backyard and double garage. Main floor: open kitchen and family room (new counters 2023), separate dining room and a two-piece bath. Upstairs: three bedrooms and a full bath. Basement partly finished with a rec room and laundry. Furnace 2021, roof 2019. Ten minutes' walk to the high school and the community pool. Annual taxes about $3,900. Request a viewing through the listing."*

## 5. Choose where to advertise it

*Use more than one place. Here's an honest comparison.*

| Option | How it works | Good for | Trade-offs |
|---|---|---|---|
| **For-sale-by-owner sites** (such as OwnersMarket, PropertyGuys, ComFree) | You pay the site a fee to advertise your home; you handle buyers yourself | Buyers specifically looking for private sales; keeping costs low | Smaller audience than the MLS; services and pricing vary, so compare what's included |
| **Flat-fee MLS listing** | You pay a brokerage a set fee to put your home on the MLS (and so on REALTOR.ca) | The widest audience, including every buyer working with an agent | Only realtors can list on the MLS, so you need a brokerage; buyers' agents will usually expect you to pay their commission |
| **Classifieds** (such as Kijiji's real estate section) | You post the home yourself | Free or low-cost local reach | Mixed audience; more spam and scam messages |
| **Social media and community groups** | Share your listing link with friends, neighbours and local buy-and-sell groups | Word of mouth; local buyers | Follow each group's rules; many limit self-promotion |
| **A lawn sign** | A sign on your property pointing to your listing | Drivers and walkers who already like the area | Local only |

**A note on the MLS.** "MLS" is a trademark of the Canadian Real Estate Association, and only realtors who are members can list homes on a board's MLS system. That's why private sellers who want MLS exposure pay a brokerage to list for them.

## 6. Put up a sign

*It's still one of the best ways to reach local buyers.*

- Use a sign that clearly says "For sale by owner" and points to your listing (a QR code or a short web address).
- You don't have to put your phone number on it; sending people to the listing keeps your contact details private and gives you their name before a viewing.
- Check any condo, strata or HOA rules about signs before you put one up.

---

## 7. Decide your approach to buyers' agents

*Decide before the first call, not during it.*

Some buyers will come with an agent, who will usually ask you to pay their commission. You can say yes, negotiate, or say no. If you're open to it, say so in your listing; on OwnersMarket, tick "Agents welcome" and say what you'd offer. Agree any commission in writing with the agent's brokerage, have your lawyer look at it, and remember it reduces what you save.

---

## 8. Share it safely

*Make it easy for real buyers and hard for scammers.*

- Keep your exact address private until you've confirmed a viewing with a named buyer.
- Answer messages quickly, but only move money through lawyers' trust accounts. Never send money, banking details or ID to someone you've met through your listing.
- Watch for "buyers" who won't view the home, offer to overpay and ask for a refund, or press for personal information.
- Protect your title: shred old documents, keep your mortgage information safe, and ask your lawyer about title insurance if you're worried about title fraud.
- If you see your photos in a fake listing (often a fake rental), report it to the site and to the Canadian Anti-Fraud Centre.

> **Warning:** money for a home sale only ever moves between the lawyers' trust accounts (or a BC notary's). Anyone asking you to do otherwise is a red flag.

---

## 9. Keep the listing fresh

*If viewing requests don't come in, change something.*

After two or three weeks with few viewing requests, look at:

- **Price:** compare again with homes that have sold nearby since you listed.
- **First photo:** is the front photo bright and inviting? Try a different angle or time of day.
- **Description:** does the first line say what makes the home worth seeing?
- **Reach:** have you shared it beyond one site, and is the sign up?

Update your listing whenever something changes (a new price, new photos, an open house), and mark it sold as soon as the sale is firm.

## Frequently asked questions

**What is the best way to list a house for sale by owner?**
Combine a for-sale-by-owner listing with strong photos, a lawn sign and local sharing. Price it close to recent sales of similar homes, write a specific description and answer buyers quickly. Most private listings that stall are overpriced or have weak photos.

**What is the best website to list a house for sale by owner in Canada?**
It depends on what you want: for-sale-by-owner sites reach buyers looking for private sales at low cost, while a flat-fee MLS listing reaches buyers working with agents (who will usually expect a commission). Compare what each option includes and costs, and consider using more than one.

**Can I list my house on the MLS without a realtor?**
Not directly. Only realtors who are members of the Canadian Real Estate Association can list on a board's MLS system. You can pay a flat-fee brokerage to list it for you.

**Should I put my address in the listing?**
Show the neighbourhood and street area so buyers know where it is, but you don't have to publish the exact address. On OwnersMarket it stays private until you confirm a viewing.

**How many photos should a house listing have?**
Usually 10 to 25: the front, every main room once, the kitchen and bathrooms, the yard and parking. Quality and coverage matter more than the number.
