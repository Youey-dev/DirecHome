# Getting your home ready to sell: staging, repairs and a pre-listing inspection

**Short answer:** most homes don't need a renovation to sell; they need to be **clean, decluttered, bright and in good repair**, because buyers decide from photos whether to visit. Fix the small things buyers notice, stage lightly with what you already own, and consider a **pre-listing home inspection** so there are no surprises. Start a few weeks before you plan to list, especially ahead of the spring market.

---

## 1. Declutter first

*Less stuff makes rooms look bigger in photos.*

- Clear kitchen and bathroom counters down to one or two items.
- Remove personal photos, fridge magnets and collections.
- Pack away out-of-season clothes, and thin out closets: buyers open them.
- Empty or tidy the garage, basement and storage areas.
- Rent a storage unit or borrow space if you need to.

---

## 2. Small fixes that matter

*Buyers notice small problems and assume big ones.*

- Burnt-out bulbs, dripping taps, running toilets, sticking doors.
- Loose handles, missing outlet covers, cracked switch plates.
- Scuffed walls: touch up or repaint in neutral colours.
- Caulking around tubs and sinks; stained grout.
- Outside: clean the front door, cut the grass or clear snow, sweep the walkway, tidy the garden beds.

For bigger problems you won't fix, be honest about them; see [Selling a house as-is](/guides/selling-a-house-as-is).

---

## 3. Staging while you live there

*Make it look like a calm, clean version of your home.*

- **Every room has one purpose:** turn the "junk room" back into a bedroom or office.
- **Furniture:** remove a piece or two from crowded rooms so buyers can walk through easily.
- **Light:** open blinds, replace dim bulbs, add a lamp to dark corners.
- **Neutral and fresh:** clean bedding, matching towels, a plant or flowers.
- **Smell:** air out the home, clean carpets, and avoid strong scents.
- **Daily reset:** keep a basket for quick clean-ups before viewings.

Professional staging is optional and can help in vacant or very personal homes; ask for a quote if you're considering it.

---

## 4. A pre-listing home inspection: worth it?

*An inspection before you list, paid for by you.*

**Why sellers do it:**

- You find problems before a buyer does, and can fix them, price for them or disclose them.
- Fewer surprises during the buyer's inspection condition.
- You can share the report with buyers (many will still get their own).

**Things to know:**

- **Licensing:** home inspectors must be licensed in BC (Consumer Protection BC) and Alberta. Check an inspector's licence where it applies.
- **Disclosure:** once you know about a problem, you know about it. Hidden defects that make the home dangerous or unfit to live in must be disclosed; talk to your lawyer about anything the report finds.
- **Rural homes:** test your well water before you list. In Ontario, Public Health Ontario tests private well water for bacteria for free.

## Frequently asked questions

**Should I stage my house to sell?**
Light staging with what you own (decluttering, rearranging, brightening) usually helps buyers picture the home. Professional staging is optional.

**Should I get a home inspection before selling?**
It's optional but can prevent surprises. You'll know what buyers will find, and can fix, price or disclose it. Use a licensed inspector where licensing applies.

**What should I fix before selling a house?**
Cheap, visible things first: bulbs, leaks, sticking doors, scuffs and the front entrance. For big repairs, weigh the cost against selling as-is with honest disclosure.

**How do I stage a home while living in it?**
Declutter, keep a quick-reset routine before viewings, give each room one purpose, and keep personal items and valuables put away.
