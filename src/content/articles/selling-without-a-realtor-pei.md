# How to sell your house privately in Prince Edward Island

**Short answer:** you can sell your own home in PEI without a real estate agent. You advertise it, show it and talk price with buyers; a PEI lawyer handles the agreement, the deposit, the deed and closing. The buyer pays a 1% real property transfer tax (first-time buyers can be exempt). Two things are different on the Island: non-resident buyers of larger or waterfront properties may need provincial approval, and your buyer's property tax assessment resets after the sale.

This guide covers what's specific to PEI. For the steps that are the same across Canada (photos, safe viewings, advertising), see our [Canada-wide guide](/guides/selling-your-home).

---

## 1. Yes, you can sell your own home on the Island

*Real estate licensing covers people who sell for others.*

Real estate agents in PEI are licensed under the province's *Real Estate Trading Act*. As the owner, you can advertise, show and sell your own home. You'll need a PEI lawyer for the legal side, as in any sale.

---

## 2. Hire your lawyer first

*Line them up before you list, so offers can go straight to them.*

Your lawyer usually:

- prepares or reviews the agreement of purchase and sale and any counter-offers
- holds the buyer's deposit in trust
- searches and confirms your title, and prepares the deed
- gets your mortgage payout and pays off your lender
- prepares the statement of adjustments (property tax, condo fees, heating oil)
- registers the deed on closing day

Ask for a fixed-fee quote. Then call your mortgage lender about any prepayment penalty: breaking a closed mortgage early can cost thousands.

> **Ask your lawyer.** Do I have a survey, and will the buyer need one? Does my property raise any Lands Protection Act issues for non-resident buyers? How would you like to receive offers?

---

## 3. Research your price

*Your assessment is a reference, not a price.*

The provincial government assesses every property on PEI. Use your assessment as one reference alongside recent sales of similar homes nearby, similar homes listed now, and a paid appraisal if you want an independent opinion. Pricing is your decision.

> **PEI note: your buyer's assessment resets.** For owner-occupied homes kept by the same owner, PEI limits yearly assessment increases to the Island's inflation rate (capped at 5%). When the home is sold, the new owner's assessment starts again from the property's full residential assessment. So if you've owned your home for years, your buyer's property tax may be higher than yours. Share your tax bill, but expect buyers to ask.

---

## 4. Non-resident buyers and the Lands Protection Act

*Mostly matters for larger, rural or waterfront properties.*

Under PEI's *Lands Protection Act*, a buyer who isn't a PEI resident (or any corporation) needs approval from the Island Regulatory and Appeals Commission (IRAC) if their total land holdings on the Island would be **more than 5 acres** or include **more than 165 feet of shore frontage**.

What it means for you:

- If your property is over 5 acres or on the water, say so in your listing so out-of-province buyers can plan for an application.
- Ask your lawyer whether the agreement should allow time for IRAC approval.
- Most homes in town on ordinary lots are well under these limits.

---

## 5. PEI documents to gather

| Document | Why PEI buyers ask for it |
|---|---|
| Property assessment and tax notice | Part of the statement of adjustments; see the assessment note above |
| Mortgage details | Your lawyer requests the payout statement |
| **Survey or plan** | Especially for waterfront or rural land, and for the Lands Protection Act acreage and frontage questions |
| **Heating oil details** | Many Island homes heat with oil; buyers and insurers ask about the tank's age and location |
| Well and septic records (rural homes) | Lenders and buyers commonly ask for a water test and septic information |
| Condo documents (condos only) | Declaration, bylaws, budget and reserve fund study |
| Permits and receipts for major work | Buyers and inspectors ask |

For the full stage-by-stage list, see our [paperwork checklist](/guides/paperwork-to-sell-your-house-privately).

**Disclosure.** PEI doesn't require private sellers to use a standard disclosure form, but across Canada, hiding a known hidden problem that makes a home dangerous or unfit to live in can lead to a lawsuit after the sale. Write down every problem you know of and go through it with your lawyer.

---

## 6. If you're married: your spouse's consent

*Even if only you are on title.*

Under PEI's *Family Law Act*, a married spouse can't sell or mortgage an interest in a family home unless the other spouse joins in the deed or consents, with a few exceptions such as a separation agreement or a court order. This part of the Act applies to married spouses, not common-law partners.

Tell your lawyer early if you're married, separated, or the home is in one name only.

Separating or divorcing? See [Selling your house during a separation or divorce](/guides/selling-your-house-during-separation-or-divorce).

---

## 7. Offers and transfer tax

*Every offer goes to your lawyer before you respond.*

The agreement of purchase and sale sets out the price, deposit, included items, conditions and the closing date. Common conditions are financing, a home inspection and, for rural homes, water and septic tests.

- You can accept, counter or decline.
- Once both sides sign and the buyer's conditions are met or removed, the sale is firm.
- PEI doesn't have a BC-style 3-day buyer cancellation right for resale homes, so get legal advice before you sign.

**Real property transfer tax (paid by the buyer).** 1% of the price or the assessed value, whichever is higher. There's no tax if that amount is $30,000 or less. **First-time buyers** can be exempt if they're Canadian citizens or permanent residents, have lived on PEI for at least six months (or filed tax returns here in two of the last six years), have never owned a home they lived in, and will live in this one. If they don't live in it for at least 183 consecutive days, the tax becomes payable.

> **Warning: deposits.** The buyer's deposit goes into a lawyer's (or brokerage's) trust account, as the agreement says. Never accept a deposit yourself, by cash, cheque or e-Transfer.

---

## 8. Closing on the Island

- A few days before closing, sign the deed (with your spouse, if married) and closing papers at your lawyer's office. Bring government photo ID.
- On closing day, the lawyers exchange documents and money, the deed is registered, and your lawyer pays off your mortgage.
- Hand over keys and codes as the agreement says; note the oil level if it's being adjusted.
- Your lawyer sends you the net proceeds and a final report.
- At tax time, report the sale on Schedule 3 and designate the home as your principal residence.

## Frequently asked questions

**Can I sell my house privately in PEI?**
Yes. Licensing covers people who sell homes for others. You'll still need a PEI lawyer for the agreement, the deed and closing.

**How much is the land transfer tax in PEI?**
1% of the price or assessed value, whichever is higher, paid by the buyer. There's no tax if that's $30,000 or less, and eligible first-time buyers can be exempt.

**Can a non-resident buy my house in PEI?**
Yes. Approval from IRAC is only needed if their total Island land holdings would exceed 5 acres or 165 feet of shore frontage. Most ordinary town lots are well under these limits.

**Will my buyer pay the same property tax as me?**
Not necessarily. Owner-occupied assessments rise by no more than PEI inflation (up to 5%) while the same owner keeps the home, but the new owner's assessment restarts from the full residential assessment.

**What does it cost to sell a house privately in PEI?**
Mainly your lawyer's fee, your mortgage discharge and any prepayment penalty, plus advertising (for example, $24.99 a month on OwnersMarket). If your buyer uses an agent, that agent may ask you to pay their commission, which would reduce your savings.
