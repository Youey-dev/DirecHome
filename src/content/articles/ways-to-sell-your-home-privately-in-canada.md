# Ways to sell your home privately in Canada, compared

**Short answer:** there are three main ways to sell your home without a full-service agent in Canada. A **for-sale-by-owner (FSBO) site** such as OwnersMarket advertises your home for a fee instead of a commission, and you deal with buyers directly. A **flat-fee MLS® listing** is a brokerage that puts your home on the MLS® for a set fee, where agents and their buyers look. **Free online classifieds** cost little but give you no support. Whichever you pick, you still need a real estate lawyer (or a notary in BC) to handle the sale.

## 2. For-sale-by-owner sites

A for-sale-by-owner site advertises your home to buyers who are looking for private sales. You set the price, show the home and talk with buyers; your lawyer handles the paperwork once you agree on a deal.

On **OwnersMarket**, it's $24.99 a month per active listing, with no commission and nothing to pay when it sells. Every listing is reviewed before it goes live. It's an advertising site, not a brokerage, so it doesn't put your home on the MLS®. It's open in every province and territory except Québec.

When you compare for-sale-by-owner sites, check:

- **The total cost**, including anything charged when the home sells.
- **Whether you can cancel** at any time, or are locked in.
- **What's included:** photos, a sign, how buyers contact you.
- **Whether listings are checked** before they go live.

**Good to know:** without the MLS®, your home won't appear on the agent listing sites where many buyers search. You'll lean more on your own advertising: a sign on the lawn, sharing the listing with friends and local groups, and the for-sale-by-owner site itself. See [how to list your house for sale by owner](/guides/how-to-list-your-house-for-sale-by-owner).

## 3. Flat-fee MLS® listings

A flat-fee MLS® service is a licensed brokerage that puts your home on the MLS® for a set fee. Only licensed brokerages can list on an MLS® system, which is why you need one for this. Most of the selling is still up to you: you show the home and talk with buyers.

**Good to know:** most buyers who find you through the MLS® have an agent, and that agent will usually expect to be paid. Budget for a buyer's agent commission, and agree on it in writing before any offer. See [selling privately when the buyer has an agent](/guides/selling-privately-when-buyer-has-an-agent).

## 4. Free online classifieds

Free classified sites reach a lot of people at little or no cost. They don't check listings closely, and you'll get more time-wasters and scam messages than on a dedicated site. Many sellers use them as an extra place to advertise, alongside a for-sale-by-owner listing, rather than on their own.

## 5. How to choose

1. **How much do you want to spend?** If the lowest cost matters most, a for-sale-by-owner site plus your own advertising costs the least.
2. **Do you need agents' buyers?** If homes like yours mostly sell through agents in your area, a flat-fee MLS® listing gets you in front of them. Budget for the buyer's agent.
3. **How much help do you want?** A discount brokerage gives you more hands-on help, for a higher fee.
4. **Can you show the home yourself?** Every option here except a brokerage expects you to do the showings and talk with buyers.

## Frequently asked questions

**What is the best way to sell my house by owner in Canada?**
It depends on what you need. If you want the lowest cost and to deal with buyers directly, a for-sale-by-owner site like OwnersMarket ($24.99 a month, no commission) fits. If you want your home on the MLS®, choose a flat-fee MLS® listing and budget for a buyer's agent.

**Can I list my house on the MLS® without a realtor?**
Not directly. Only licensed brokerages can put homes on an MLS® system. A flat-fee MLS® service is a brokerage that does it for a set fee.

**Can I sell my house on free classified sites?**
Yes, many sellers post there as well as on a for-sale-by-owner site. Check each site's current rules, and expect more spam and scam messages. You'll still pay your lawyer, as with any sale.

**Do I still need a lawyer if I sell privately?**
Yes. A real estate lawyer (or, in BC, a lawyer or notary) handles the offer paperwork, the deposit, the title transfer and paying off your mortgage. See [what a real estate lawyer does when you sell](/guides/what-a-real-estate-lawyer-does-when-you-sell).
