# Selling privately vs a discount brokerage vs a flat-fee MLS listing: an honest comparison

**Short answer:** there are four main ways to sell a home in Canada. **Selling privately** costs the least and asks the most of you. A **flat-fee MLS listing** gets your home in front of agents and their buyers for a set fee, but you still do most of the work. A **discount brokerage** gives you an agent for a lower commission, with fewer services. A **full-service agent** does the most, for the highest cost. Commissions are negotiable in every case, and the right choice depends on your home, your time and your market.

> **A note on fairness.** OwnersMarket is one of the private-sale options below. We've tried to describe every option honestly, including when another one would suit you better. Services and prices vary between companies and change often, so compare current offers yourself.

---

## 1. The four options at a glance

| | Selling privately | Flat-fee MLS listing | Discount brokerage | Full-service agent |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **What you pay for selling** | An advertising fee (a for-sale-by-owner site, sign, photos) | A set fee to a brokerage to list on the MLS | A reduced commission or flat fee | A commission, usually a percentage of the price |
| **Buyer's agent commission** | Only if you agree to pay it | Usually expected | Usually included or expected | Usually included in the commission |
| **Who sets the price** | You | You | You, with the agent's input | You, with the agent's advice |
| **Who shows the home** | You | Usually you | Varies by brokerage | The agent |
| **Who negotiates** | You (your lawyer handles the legal side) | Usually you | Varies | The agent |
| **Exposure** | For-sale-by-owner sites, classifieds, signs, local sharing | The MLS and REALTOR.ca, plus your own advertising | The MLS and the brokerage's marketing | The MLS and the agent's marketing |
| **Your lawyer** | Needed | Needed | Needed | Needed |

In every option, a real estate lawyer (or, in BC, a lawyer or notary) handles the legal transfer. That cost is the same whichever way you sell.

## 2. What each one costs (an example)

*Commissions aren't set by law and are negotiable. These are examples, not quotes.*

The Financial Consumer Agency of Canada notes that commissions are negotiable and typically range from about 2% to 6%, depending on where you live. To compare, here's a common example structure: **7% on the first $100,000 plus 2.5% on the rest, plus 5% GST**.

| On a $650,000 home | Estimate |
|---|---|
| Full commission at the example rate | about **$21,788** |
| Listing side (about half) | about $10,894 |
| Buyer's agent side (about half) | about $10,894 |

How that maps to each option, roughly:

- **Full-service agent:** the full commission, about $21,788 in this example (often split with the buyer's agent).
- **Discount brokerage:** a lower listing side (a reduced percentage or a flat fee), plus the buyer's agent side, which is usually still expected.
- **Flat-fee MLS listing:** a one-time brokerage fee, plus the buyer's agent side if the buyer has an agent, which is common on the MLS.
- **Selling privately:** your advertising costs, plus the buyer's agent side **only if** you agree to pay it. On OwnersMarket, for example, advertising is $24.99 a month.

The buyer's agent's commission is often the biggest single cost left after you cut the listing side, so it's worth deciding your approach early (see [Selling privately when the buyer has an agent](/guides/selling-privately-when-buyer-has-an-agent)).

> **Estimate only.** Commissions are negotiable and vary by province. If your buyer uses an agent, that agent may ask you to pay their commission, which would reduce your savings. Try the [calculator](/calculator) for your own numbers.

## 3. Selling privately

**How it works:** you advertise the home yourself on for-sale-by-owner sites (such as OwnersMarket, PropertyGuys or ComFree), with a lawn sign and local sharing. You show it, talk to buyers and agree the price. Your lawyer handles the offer paperwork, the deposit and closing.

**Good fit if:** your home is move-in ready, similar homes nearby sell regularly, you can open the door for viewings, and you're comfortable talking price.

**Watch out for:** less exposure to buyers who only search the MLS; pricing and negotiating are on you; and you'll need a plan for buyers who come with an agent.

---

## 4. Flat-fee MLS listing

**How it works:** you pay a brokerage a set fee to put your home on the MLS (and so on REALTOR.ca). Only realtors who are members of the Canadian Real Estate Association can list on an MLS system, which is why a brokerage is needed. You typically still show the home and negotiate yourself; extra services may cost more.

**Good fit if:** you want the biggest possible audience, including every buyer working with an agent, but you're happy to do the selling yourself.

**Watch out for:**

- **Buyers' agents will usually expect a commission,** because they found your home on the MLS. Check what the listing says you'll offer.
- **You're signing an agreement with a brokerage.** Read what's included, how long it lasts, how to end it, and whether there's a "holdover" clause that charges you if a buyer who saw the home during the listing buys after it ends.

---

## 5. Discount brokerage

**How it works:** a licensed brokerage represents you for a lower commission or a flat fee. What's included varies: some offer nearly full service, others leave showings or open houses to you.

**Good fit if:** you want an agent to advise on price and negotiate, but don't want to pay a full commission.

**Watch out for:** services vary a lot, so get the list in writing. Ontario's regulator, RECO, puts it plainly: there's no standard set of services, and you shouldn't assume a service is included unless it's written in the agreement. The buyer's agent's commission is usually still on top.

---

## 6. Full-service agent

**How it works:** an agent advises on price, prepares and markets the listing, shows the home, negotiates offers and manages the sale until closing.

**Good fit if:** you can't be there for viewings, the home is unusual or hard to price, you need to sell by a fixed date, or you simply want someone else to manage it.

**Watch out for:** it's the highest-cost option. Commissions are negotiable: ask what's included, and compare more than one agent.

---

## 7. How to choose

Answer these honestly:

1. **Can I be home for viewings?** If not, an agent (discount or full-service) is the practical choice.
2. **Is my home easy to compare with recent sales nearby?** If yes, pricing it yourself is realistic. If not, consider an appraisal or an agent's advice (see [How to research your asking price](/guides/how-to-research-your-asking-price)).
3. **How much does reaching MLS-only buyers matter in my market?** If most buyers in your area search with agents, a flat-fee MLS listing may be worth its cost.
4. **Am I comfortable negotiating price directly?** If not, an agent will do it for you.
5. **What's at stake?** Run your numbers in the [calculator](/calculator).

**You can change your mind.** Many sellers try privately first, then move to a flat-fee or agent listing if it isn't working. If you do sign with a brokerage later, read the agreement for any clause about buyers who saw your home before.

## Frequently asked questions

**What's the least expensive way to sell a house in Canada?**
Selling privately usually costs the least, because there's no listing commission; you pay to advertise, plus your lawyer (as in any sale). If your buyer has an agent and you agree to pay them, that adds to the cost.

**Is a flat-fee MLS listing worth it?**
It can be, if you want maximum exposure to buyers working with agents and are happy to show and negotiate yourself. Expect to pay the buyer's agent commission on most MLS sales, and read the brokerage agreement carefully.

**What's the difference between a discount brokerage and a flat-fee MLS listing?**
A discount brokerage usually represents you as an agent, for a reduced fee, with some services included. A flat-fee MLS listing is mainly about getting your home on the MLS; you usually do most of the selling yourself. The exact services depend on the company.

**Can I sell privately and still use an agent later?**
Yes. Many sellers start privately and switch if it isn't working. If you sign a listing agreement, check its holdover clause so you know what happens with buyers who saw the home earlier.

**Do I need a lawyer with every option?**
Yes. Whichever way you sell, a lawyer (or, in BC, a lawyer or notary) handles the legal transfer, the deposit and your mortgage payout.
