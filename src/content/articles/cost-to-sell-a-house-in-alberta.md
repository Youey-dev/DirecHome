# How much does it cost to sell a house in Alberta?

**Short answer:** the biggest cost of selling a house in Alberta is usually the real estate commission, plus 5% GST on it. Commissions aren't set by law and are negotiable. Alberta sellers also pay a lawyer, a mortgage discharge fee and possibly a prepayment penalty, and usually the cost of a current **Real Property Report** with municipal compliance. Alberta has no land transfer tax; the buyer pays small Land Titles fees.

> **Estimates only.** The commission figures below use an example rate, not a standard one. Your costs depend on your price, your agreement and your buyer.

## 2. Real estate commission and GST

*Negotiable, plus 5% GST.*

Commissions aren't set by law. The Financial Consumer Agency of Canada says they're negotiable and typically range from about 2% to 6%, depending on where you live. In Alberta, commission is subject to **5% GST**; Alberta has no provincial sales tax.

Here's an **example only**, using a common structure: 7% on the first $100,000 plus 2.5% on the rest, plus GST.

| On a $500,000 Alberta home | Estimate |
|---|---|
| Commission at the example rate | $17,000 |
| GST (5%) | $850 |
| **Total** | **$17,850** |
| Usually split: listing side | about $8,925 |
| Usually split: buyer's agent side | about $8,925 |

Use the [commission calculator](/calculator) to try your own numbers.

**If you sell privately:** there's no listing side. If your buyer has an agent, that agent may ask you to pay their commission. You can agree, negotiate or decline; see [Selling privately when the buyer has an agent](/guides/selling-privately-when-buyer-has-an-agent).

## 3. Your lawyer

*Every Alberta sale closes through lawyers.*

Your Alberta lawyer reviews the contract, handles the deposit, pays out your mortgage, prepares the transfer and dower documents and works with the buyer's lawyer to register the sale at Land Titles. Fees vary, and there's no official rate, so get two or three written quotes, with disbursements listed separately.

---

## 4. The Real Property Report

*Alberta's extra seller cost, and the one to start early.*

Most Alberta buyers, their lawyers and lenders expect the seller to provide a current **Real Property Report (RPR)**, prepared by an Alberta Land Surveyor, with your municipality's **compliance** stamp.

- **If you have a recent RPR** and nothing has changed (no new deck, fence, garage or driveway), you may only need compliance.
- **If things have changed,** ask the surveyor who did the original about an update, which is often cheaper than a new report.
- **Costs vary** by surveyor, lot and municipality, and municipalities charge their own compliance fee. Get a quote, and order it before you list, because compliance can take a few weeks.
- If the RPR or compliance review turns up a problem, such as a shed over the property line, fixing it is a cost too.

---

## 5. Your mortgage: discharge and any penalty

- **Discharge fee:** most lenders charge one, plus a small Land Titles registration cost; your lawyer pays them from the proceeds.
- **Prepayment penalty:** breaking a closed mortgage early usually costs the higher of three months' interest or the interest rate differential (IRD), and it can be thousands. Ask your lender for a written estimate, and ask about **porting** the mortgage.

More in [Selling a house with a mortgage](/guides/selling-a-house-with-a-mortgage).

---

## 6. Condo sellers: the estoppel certificate

The **estoppel certificate** confirms your condo fees and anything owing. The corporation must provide it within 10 days of a written request, for up to **$200** (a rush fee may apply if its bylaws allow). Buyers will also review the bylaws, budget, reserve fund study and minutes.

---

## 7. Taxes and fees: who pays what in Alberta

- **No land transfer tax.** The buyer pays Land Titles fees instead: $50 plus $5 for every $5,000 of value for the transfer ($550 on a $500,000 home), and the same formula on their mortgage.
- **Income tax:** usually none on your principal residence if it was your home every year you owned it, but you must report the sale on your tax return.

## 8. Comparing the ways to sell

| On the $500,000 example | Commission + GST you pay |
|---|---|
| Full-service agent at the example rate | about $17,850 |
| Selling privately, buyer has no agent | $0 commission (plus advertising) |
| Selling privately, you agree to pay the buyer's agent side | about $8,925 |

Your lawyer, RPR, mortgage costs and moving apply in every case. For the full comparison, see [Private sale vs discount brokerage vs flat-fee MLS](/guides/selling-privately-vs-discount-brokerage-vs-flat-fee-mls).

## Frequently asked questions

**What are realtor fees in Alberta?**
There's no set fee. Commissions are negotiable, usually a percentage of the price (sometimes higher on the first $100,000), plus 5% GST. Ask for the rate and what's included, in writing.

**Who pays for the Real Property Report in Alberta?**
Usually the seller, because standard Alberta contracts expect the seller to provide a current RPR with compliance. Confirm with your lawyer what your agreement says.

**Is there a land transfer tax in Alberta?**
No. The buyer pays Land Titles fees: $50 plus $5 for every $5,000 of value on the transfer, and the same on their mortgage.

**What are seller closing costs in Alberta?**
Your lawyer's fee and disbursements, your RPR and compliance, your mortgage discharge fee and any prepayment penalty, an estoppel certificate for a condo, and moving. Commission and GST are extra if you use an agent.
