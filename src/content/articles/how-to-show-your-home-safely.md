# How to show your home safely when you're selling it yourself

**Short answer:** know who's coming before you share your address, have another adult home for every viewing, put away valuables, medication and personal documents, and show one group at a time. Never move money yourself: deposits and sale money only go through lawyers' trust accounts. Most viewers are exactly who they say they are; a few simple habits deal with the rest.

---

## 1. Before you share your address: know who's coming

*A name and a phone number before the viewing, every time.*

- **Get their full name and a working phone number.** On OwnersMarket, your exact address stays private until you confirm a viewing, so you'll have the buyer's name and messages on record first.
- **Call or text back on the number they gave.** A real buyer picks up or replies; a fake one often only wants to chat in the app.
- **Ask a couple of easy questions:** "Are you working with a lender?" "Are you selling a home too?" "When are you hoping to move?" You don't need documents; you're getting a feel for whether they're a real buyer.
- **If they have an agent,** confirm the agent's name and brokerage, and settle any commission question first (see [Selling privately when the buyer has an agent](/guides/selling-privately-when-buyer-has-an-agent)).
- **Trust your instincts.** You can say no to a viewing. A polite "That time doesn't work, I'll let you know if something opens up" is enough.

---

## 2. Get the home ready, safely

*What a stranger might see, pick up or read.*

Put away or lock up:

- **valuables:** jewellery, cash, small electronics, collectibles
- **medication:** bathroom cabinets and bedside tables are opened at viewings
- **personal documents:** mail, bills, bank statements, passports, anything with your account numbers or signature
- **keys and garage remotes,** including spare keys on hooks
- **photos and details that identify your children** (school names, schedules on the fridge)

Also:

- **Pets:** arrange for them to be out, or crated in one room you tell viewers about.
- **Walkways:** clear and salt them in winter, and make sure stairs and railings are solid. You want visitors leaving the way they arrived.
- **Cameras:** if you have security cameras, tell viewers. Recording people's conversations can raise privacy issues, so video only is the safer choice.
- **Insurance:** if you're unsure, ask your home insurer whether anything changes while the home is being shown.

---

## 3. During the viewing

*Two of you, one group at a time, you lead the way.*

- **Have another adult home with you.** If that's not possible, tell someone the viewer's name and the time, and text them when it's over.
- **Show one group at a time,** at a time you chose.
- **Let them walk ahead and you follow,** room to room, rather than letting people wander alone. Stay near the exit in small spaces like basements and closets.
- **Keep your phone charged and with you.**
- **Answer questions about the home honestly.** For questions about the agreement, the deposit or the law, say "my lawyer can answer that".
- **Don't share your bottom line** (how quickly you need to sell, or the lowest price you'd take). Talk price in writing, through an offer.
- **End it politely if anything feels off.** "I'm going to wrap up now, thanks for coming" is all you need.

---

## 4. Open houses

*More people, so more structure.*

- **Have at least two adults** for the whole open house.
- **Use a sign-in sheet** with names and phone numbers, and ask people to sign in as they arrive.
- **Limit numbers** in the home at once; ask others to wait outside or in one room.
- **Lock rooms you're not showing,** or close them and say they're private.
- **Afterwards,** walk through the whole home: check every window and door is locked, and that nothing has been moved or unlatched.

---

## 5. The scams that target private sellers

*Nearly all of them end with you sending money or personal information.*

| Red flag | What's usually going on |
|---|---|
| A buyer who won't view the home but wants to buy quickly, often from abroad | Setting up an overpayment or deposit scam |
| They send "too much" for the deposit and ask you to refund the difference | The original payment will bounce; your refund won't |
| They ask for your banking details, SIN, ID or a copy of your deed | Identity theft, often the first step to title fraud |
| An email "from your lawyer" with new payment instructions | Payment redirection fraud: someone impersonating the lawyer |
| Someone wants to "inspect" or "appraise" your home with no buyer attached | Scouting the home, or setting up a fake fee |
| Your photos turn up in a different listing, often a rental | A fake rental scam using your home |

How to protect yourself:

- **Money only moves through lawyers' trust accounts** (or a BC notary's). Never accept a deposit yourself, and never send money to anyone you met through your listing.
- **Verify payment instructions independently.** If you get new banking details by email, call your lawyer on the number you already have, not the one in the email.
- **Protect your title:** shred old documents, keep your mortgage information safe, and ask your lawyer about title insurance if you're worried.
- **Report it:** to the site where you saw it, and to the Canadian Anti-Fraud Centre. If you've lost money or your identity has been used, also contact your bank and police.

> **Warning:** a real buyer never needs your banking details, your SIN or a refund from you. If someone asks, stop and talk to your lawyer.

---

## 6. After an offer arrives

*Safety doesn't end at the viewing.*

- Send every offer to your lawyer before you respond.
- Second viewings and inspections: same rules, another adult home, and keep valuables away.
- On possession day, hand over every key and remote, and change any codes you've shared with contractors or family.

## Frequently asked questions

**How do I do a showing without a realtor?**
Confirm the buyer's name and phone number, prepare the home, have another adult with you, and walk the buyer through room by room. Answer questions about the home honestly and send any legal or contract questions to your lawyer.

**Is it safe to hold an open house when selling privately?**
It can be, with two adults present, a sign-in sheet, a limit on how many people are inside at once, and private rooms locked. Check every door and window afterwards.

**Should I ask buyers for proof of pre-approval before a viewing?**
You can ask whether they're working with a lender, and many buyers will tell you. You don't need to collect documents; a conversation and a confirmed phone number are usually enough to filter out time-wasters.

**What are the most common scams on private home sales?**
Overpayment and refund scams, requests for your banking or ID details, emails impersonating your lawyer with new payment instructions, and fake rental listings using your photos. The common thread is moving money or personal information outside the lawyers' trust accounts.
