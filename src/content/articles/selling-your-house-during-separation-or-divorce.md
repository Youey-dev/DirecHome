# Selling your house during a separation or divorce

**Short answer:** in most provinces, a married spouse has rights in the family home even if their name isn't on the title, so expect **both spouses to sign** the sale. Agree the important things in writing first: whether to sell, the price, who handles the sale and how the money will be held and divided. Each spouse should have their own family lawyer. The sale itself works like any other, and selling privately can leave more money for both of you.

> **This is general information, not legal advice.** Family property rules are complex and differ by province. Talk to a family lawyer before you list.

---

## 1. Who has to sign? Spousal rights by province

*Being on the title isn't the whole story.*

| Province | The rule in short |
|---|---|
| **BC** | No automatic consent rule, but a spouse can register an entry under the *Land (Spouse Protection) Act*; after that, the owner can't sell without consent. Family property is generally shared on separation. |
| **Alberta** | Under the *Dower Act*, a married spouse's written consent (or a dower release or court order) is needed to sell the homestead. |
| **Saskatchewan** | Under the *Homesteads Act, 1989*, both spouses must consent, including common-law partners (until 24 months after separation). |
| **Ontario** | Under the *Family Law Act*, a married spouse usually signs consent for the sale of a matrimonial home, even if not on title. |
| **Nova Scotia** | Under the *Matrimonial Property Act*, a spouse's consent is needed (married spouses and registered domestic partners). |
| **New Brunswick** | Under the *Marital Property Act*, the other married spouse must join in the deed. |
| **PEI** | Under the *Family Law Act*, a married spouse must join in or consent. |
| **Newfoundland and Labrador** | The matrimonial home is treated as owned equally by married spouses, whatever the deed says. |

Each province guide has the details and sources: [BC](/guides/selling-without-a-realtor-bc), [Alberta](/guides/selling-without-a-realtor-alberta), [Saskatchewan](/guides/selling-without-a-realtor-saskatchewan), [Ontario](/guides/selling-without-a-realtor-ontario), [Nova Scotia](/guides/selling-without-a-realtor-nova-scotia), [New Brunswick](/guides/selling-without-a-realtor-new-brunswick), [PEI](/guides/selling-without-a-realtor-pei), [Newfoundland and Labrador](/guides/selling-without-a-realtor-newfoundland-and-labrador).

> **Warning:** selling or mortgaging the family home without the other spouse's required consent can be set aside by a court. Don't try to sell around your spouse.

## 2. Agree the plan in writing first

*Most stalled sales come from disagreements, not the market.*

Before you list, agree (ideally in a written agreement your lawyers review):

- **Whether to sell,** or whether one spouse will buy the other out
- **How to set the price:** for example, an independent appraisal, or the method in [How to research your asking price](/guides/how-to-research-your-asking-price)
- **Who handles the sale:** listing, viewings and talking to buyers
- **How offers are decided:** for example, both must agree, or accept any offer above an agreed amount
- **Who lives in the home** and pays the mortgage, taxes and insurance until closing
- **Where the money goes:** often it's held in a lawyer's trust account until the property division is settled

---

## 3. Taxes

*Usually none on a family home that was your principal residence, but check.*

- If the home was your **principal residence** for every year you owned it, the gain is generally exempt, but the sale must be reported on your tax return.
- Since 1982, a **family unit** (you and your spouse) can generally designate only **one** principal residence per year. If you own a second property, like a cottage or rental, talk to an accountant.
- If one spouse moved out and the home was rented, or one spouse kept it for years before selling, different rules can apply.

---

## 4. Running the sale smoothly

- **One point of contact** for buyers, agreed by both spouses, avoids mixed messages.
- **Viewings:** agree times and who will be home.
- **Keep the home presentable** for photos and viewings, even if it's a hard time.
- **Offers go to both lawyers** if each of you has one; agree in advance how quickly you'll respond.
- **Disclosure:** both spouses should agree what's disclosed about the home's known problems.

---

## 5. Is selling privately a good idea in a separation?

It can be: there's no listing commission to split, so more equity is left for both of you. It works best when you can cooperate on viewings and decisions. If communication is difficult, an agent (or a clear written process) may reduce conflict.

## Frequently asked questions

**Can my spouse sell the house without my permission?**
In most provinces, not if you're married and it's the family home: your consent or signature is usually required, even if you're not on the title. BC is different unless a spousal entry is registered on the title. Talk to a family lawyer.

**Do we pay capital gains tax when we sell the house in a divorce?**
Usually not on a home that was your principal residence every year you owned it, but the sale must be reported. A second property, or years of renting the home, can change that.

**Should we sell before or after the divorce is final?**
It depends on your agreement, finances and the market. Many couples sell during separation once they've agreed how to divide the proceeds. Your family lawyers can advise on timing.

**Where does the money go when the house is sold?**
Often it's held in a lawyer's trust account until property division is settled, or divided as your written agreement says.
