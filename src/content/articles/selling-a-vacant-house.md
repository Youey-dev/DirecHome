# Selling a vacant house: insurance, taxes and showings

**Short answer:** selling an empty home is common (after a move, an inheritance or a job transfer), but it brings three extra jobs: **tell your insurer**, because coverage can be limited for unoccupied homes; **check vacancy taxes** where you live, such as BC's speculation and vacancy tax or Toronto's Vacant Home Tax; and **keep the home safe and maintained**, especially in winter. If you've moved away, plan how viewings will work before you list.

---

## 1. Insurance: call your insurer first

*The most important step, and the easiest to forget.*

The Insurance Bureau of Canada notes that coverage may be more limited when a property is left unoccupied for long periods, and that losses like frozen pipes could fall outside coverage. Insurers commonly expect owners to inspect, maintain and winterize a home that isn't being lived in.

- **Tell your insurer** the home is empty and for sale, and ask what they require (for example, how often it must be checked, and whether the heat must stay on or the water be shut off).
- **Get it in writing,** and keep a simple log of each check.
- **Ask about vacancy coverage** if your regular policy limits it.

---

## 2. Vacancy taxes

*Some provinces and cities tax homes that sit empty.*

- **BC:** the **speculation and vacancy tax** applies in designated areas of BC. Owners must declare each year and claim any exemption that applies. Check the province's exemptions for your situation (for example, the year an heir inherits).
- **Toronto:** the **Vacant Home Tax** is 3% of the property's assessed value, and residential owners must declare their occupancy status every year. A home that changed ownership during the year can qualify for an exemption.
- **Other municipalities** may have their own vacancy taxes. Check with your city.

Don't miss a declaration just because you're selling: a missed one can trigger a tax or penalty.

---

## 3. Keep the home safe and maintained

- **Heat and water:** follow your insurer's instructions. In winter, frozen pipes are the classic vacant-home disaster.
- **Regular checks:** visit (or have someone visit) on a schedule; look for leaks, pests and break-ins.
- **Look lived-in:** keep the lawn cut or snow cleared, collect flyers, and use timers on a few lights.
- **Utilities:** keep power on for viewings and heat; tell utility companies your sale date.
- **Remove valuables** and anything that could tempt a break-in.

---

## 4. Showing a vacant home

*Empty homes can look smaller and colder; small touches help.*

- **Clean thoroughly** and fix small things; empty rooms show every mark.
- **Light and warmth:** turn on all lights and set a comfortable temperature for viewings.
- **Light staging:** a few pieces (a table, chairs, a rug) help buyers judge room sizes. It's optional.
- **Photos first:** take your listing photos when the home is clean, bright and tidy.

**If you've moved away:**

- Ask a trusted person nearby to open the door for viewings; [How to show your home safely](/guides/how-to-show-your-home-safely) still applies.
- Never leave a key under a mat or share a lockbox code with someone you haven't confirmed.
- Your lawyer can handle signing remotely in many cases; ask early.

---

## 5. Costs while it's empty

Mortgage payments, property tax, insurance, utilities and upkeep continue until closing. If you've bought your next home, these are carrying costs on two homes; see [Selling a house with a mortgage](/guides/selling-a-house-with-a-mortgage) for bridge financing and timing.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is it harder to sell a vacant house?**
Not necessarily. Empty homes are easy to show at short notice. Clean, well-lit rooms and a few staging pieces help buyers picture living there.

**Do I need special insurance for a vacant house?**
Possibly. Coverage can be limited for unoccupied homes. Tell your insurer it's empty and for sale, and follow their requirements in writing.

**Do I have to pay a vacancy tax while selling?**
It depends where the home is. BC's speculation and vacancy tax and Toronto's Vacant Home Tax both require yearly declarations. Check whether an exemption applies.

**Can I sell my house if I've already moved to another province?**
Yes. Arrange a trusted local contact for viewings, and ask your lawyer about signing documents remotely.
