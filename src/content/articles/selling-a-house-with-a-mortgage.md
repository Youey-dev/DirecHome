# Selling a house with a mortgage still on it

**Short answer:** yes, you can sell a house that still has a mortgage. Most Canadian sellers do. On closing day your lawyer (or, in BC, your lawyer or notary) uses the buyer's money to pay off your mortgage, then sends you what's left. The things to sort out early are your **payout amount**, any **prepayment penalty** for breaking the mortgage before its term ends, and whether you can **port** the mortgage to your next home.

---

## 1. How the payout works

*Your mortgage is paid from the sale money, not from your pocket.*

1. **Before closing,** your lawyer asks your lender for a **payout statement**: the exact amount needed to pay off the mortgage on closing day, including interest, any penalty and fees.
2. **On closing day,** the buyer's money arrives at your lawyer, who sends the payout amount to your lender.
3. **After closing,** the lender issues a discharge, and your lawyer makes sure it's registered so the mortgage comes off the title.
4. **You receive the rest:** the sale price, minus the mortgage payout, adjustments, legal fees and any other costs.

If you also have a **home equity line of credit (HELOC)** secured on the home, it has to be paid off too. Tell your lawyer about every loan registered against the property.

---

## 2. The prepayment penalty

*The cost most sellers don't see coming.*

If you have a **closed** mortgage and sell before the end of its term, your lender will usually charge a **prepayment penalty**. The Financial Consumer Agency of Canada explains that it's usually the **higher of**:

- **three months' interest** on the amount you owe, or
- the **interest rate differential (IRD)**: roughly, the interest the lender loses because today's rates are lower than yours.

Lenders often use the IRD if your rate is higher than current rates. It can cost thousands of dollars. An **open** mortgage can usually be paid off without a penalty.

**What to do:** call your lender **before you set your price** and ask for a written estimate of the penalty and the discharge fee on your expected closing date. Your mortgage agreement also explains how the penalty is calculated.

---

## 3. Ways to reduce or avoid the penalty

- **Port the mortgage:** many mortgages let you move your rate and term to your next home. Ask about the time limit between selling and buying, and what happens if the new mortgage is bigger.
- **Use your prepayment privileges:** some mortgages let you pay down a set amount each year without penalty, which lowers the balance the penalty is based on. Some lenders restrict this close to a break.
- **Time the sale:** if your term ends soon, closing after the renewal date may avoid the penalty entirely.
- **Ask about the buyer taking it over (assumption):** some mortgages allow a qualified buyer to assume yours. It's less common, and the buyer has to qualify with your lender.

---

## 4. Selling and buying at the same time

*Line up the dates, or plan for the gap.*

- **Same-day closings:** many people close the sale and the purchase on the same day, so the sale money funds the new purchase. Your lawyer coordinates it.
- **Buy first, sell later:** your lender may offer **bridge financing**, a short-term loan that covers the down payment until your sale closes. It costs interest and fees.
- **Sell first, buy later:** no bridge loan needed, but you may need somewhere to live in between. If you're porting, check your lender's time limit.

> **Ask your lender.** What's my penalty if I close on [date]? Can I port, and for how long? Do you offer bridge financing, and what does it cost?

---

## 5. What if I owe more than the home sells for?

If your sale price won't cover the mortgage, penalty and costs, you'll need to bring the difference to closing, or agree another arrangement with your lender **before** you accept an offer. Talk to your lender and lawyer early; don't sign a sale you can't close.

---

## 6. Selling privately with a mortgage

Nothing about the mortgage changes when you sell without an agent. Your lawyer handles the payout and discharge exactly as in an agent-listed sale. What you save on commission stays with you after the mortgage is paid.

## Frequently asked questions

**Can I sell my house if I still owe on the mortgage?**
Yes. Your lawyer pays off the mortgage from the sale money on closing day, and you receive what's left.

**How much is the penalty for breaking a mortgage when selling?**
It depends on your mortgage. For a closed mortgage it's usually the higher of three months' interest or the interest rate differential, and it can be thousands. Ask your lender for a written estimate.

**Can I take my mortgage to my new home?**
Many mortgages are portable, which can avoid the penalty. Ask your lender about the conditions and time limit.

**Who pays off the mortgage when I sell?**
Your lawyer (or, in BC, your lawyer or notary), using the buyer's money on closing day. It comes out of your proceeds.
