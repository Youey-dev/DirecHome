# How long does it take to sell a house privately?

**Short answer:** a private sale has four stages: **getting ready** (usually a few weeks), **finding a buyer** (anywhere from days to months, mostly depending on price and your local market), **conditions** (set in the agreement, often a week or two), and **closing** (the date you and the buyer agree, often a month or more after the offer). The finding-a-buyer stage varies the most. Pricing close to recent sales and strong photos are what shorten it.

> **No averages here on purpose.** How long homes take to sell changes with the market, the season and the neighbourhood. Recent sales near you are a better guide than any national number.

---

## Stage 1: Getting ready (usually a few weeks)

*Mostly in your control.*

- Hire your lawyer (or BC notary) and call your lender about any penalty.
- Gather documents; order anything that takes time.
- Clean, declutter, make small repairs and take photos.
- Research your price.

**Things that take longer than people expect:**

- **Alberta:** a Real Property Report and municipal compliance can take a few weeks. See the [Alberta guide](/guides/selling-without-a-realtor-alberta).
- **Condos:** strata and condo certificates have legal deadlines of 7 to 10 days in several provinces; order early.
- **Estates:** probate can take months. See [Selling an inherited house](/guides/selling-an-inherited-house).

---

## Stage 2: Finding a buyer (days to months)

*The stage that varies most.*

What speeds it up:

- **Price close to recent sales** of similar homes. Overpricing is the most common reason private listings sit.
- **Bright, complete photos** and an honest, specific description.
- **Being flexible** for viewings, including evenings and weekends.
- **Reaching more buyers:** a for-sale-by-owner listing, a lawn sign, local sharing, and an open door to buyers' agents if you choose.
- **Season:** many markets are busier in spring; winter can be slower.

If viewing requests are slow after two or three weeks, review your price, first photo and description. See [How to list your house for sale by owner](/guides/how-to-list-your-house-for-sale-by-owner).

---

## Stage 3: Offer to firm sale (often a week or two)

*Set by the agreement you sign.*

- **Negotiation:** offers and counter-offers can take a day or several days.
- **Conditions (subjects in BC):** the buyer has the number of days written in the agreement to arrange financing, an inspection, condo document review and so on. Your lawyer explains the deadlines.
- **BC only:** after you accept, the buyer has **3 business days** to cancel (the rescission period), even in a private sale.
- **Firm:** once conditions are removed in writing, the sale is firm.

---

## Stage 4: Firm sale to closing (the date you agree)

*Chosen by you and the buyer.*

The closing date (completion in BC) is negotiated in the offer. Buyers need time for their mortgage and moving; you need time to move out. Many sales close a month or more after the offer, but it can be shorter or longer if both sides agree.

**Things that can add time before closing:**

- **Nova Scotia:** if your property must be converted to the land registration system, the province suggests allowing one or two extra weeks. See the [Nova Scotia guide](/guides/selling-without-a-realtor-nova-scotia).
- **New Brunswick:** converting a property to Land Titles on sale. See the [New Brunswick guide](/guides/selling-without-a-realtor-new-brunswick).
- **Newfoundland and Labrador:** title searches in a registry of deeds can take longer. See the [Newfoundland and Labrador guide](/guides/selling-without-a-realtor-newfoundland-and-labrador).
- **Tenants:** notice periods if the buyer wants vacant possession. See [Selling a house with tenants](/guides/selling-a-house-with-tenants).
- **Non-resident sellers:** the CRA certificate process. See the [paperwork checklist](/guides/paperwork-to-sell-your-house-privately).

## Frequently asked questions

**How long does it take to sell a house once an offer is accepted?**
It depends on the conditions and the closing date in the agreement. Conditions often take a week or two; closing is the date you and the buyer agree, often a month or more later.

**How long does it take to sell a house privately vs with an agent?**
The legal stages (conditions, closing) are the same. The difference is in finding a buyer, which depends mostly on price, presentation and reach, not on who does the selling.

**What's the fastest way to sell a house privately?**
Price it close to recent sales, take bright and complete photos, be flexible for viewings, have your lawyer ready, and consider welcoming buyers' agents.

**Why isn't my house selling?**
Usually the price, the photos or the reach. Check recent sales nearby, improve your first photo and description, and share your listing more widely.
